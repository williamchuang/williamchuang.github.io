How the λ-secure “self-laundromat” works

The core idea is simple: I provide the geometry and the machinery; you keep the key. The key is a secret transform T that only you know.

Step 1

You keep the secret T

  • You run a small setup routine (or the MIA VM) that embeds your documents into the λ-geometry space.
  • You choose a secret Lorentz / orthogonal transform T on your side.
  • The system applies T to both the model and your embedded data, producing T·M and T·X.
Step 2

Training happens in the T-frame

  • Training runs either on:
    • your own GPU/on-prem server, or
    • GPUs you rent in your own cloud account, using the MIA VM.
  • Only T·M and T·X ever touch that compute environment.
  • I never see your raw data or a canonical version of your tuned model.
Step 3

Inference stays vendor-blind

  • You can keep everything in the T-frame (simple, operationally clean), or locally apply T^{-1} if needed.
  • Even if someone copies the VM or model weights, they obtain only T·M', not a usable canonical twin.
  • Standard crypto (disk encryption, TLS, TEEs) adds another layer below the λ-geometry obfuscation.

Plain English You get a self-service “encrypted AI laundromat”: your data and model go in wearing your secret disguise T; all training happens in that disguise; only you can reverse it, and only if you choose to.

Under the hood: CEAS, finite-lift, GRAIL, and MIA

The landing page is simple on purpose. Underneath, the stack is built from research-grade components that you can inspect in detail.

Core pillars
  • CEAS: critical-entropy attention scaling; treats β as a controlled parameter to cut training steps and improve stability.
  • Finite-machine lift: decomposes model behavior into cycles and transients for traceability and safe edit-without-full-retrain.
  • GRAIL: geometry-native secure execution aimed at cryptomorphic (function-identical, weight-distinct) twins.
  • MIA: metric-invariant architecture where inputs, machine state, and outputs move together under group action.
