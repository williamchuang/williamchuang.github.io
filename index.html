  • This keeps the security properties while aiming to minimize extra overhead at inference time compared with a pure wrap-only approach.

How the λ-secure “self-laundromat” works

The core idea is simple: the geometry and machinery come from Logarchéon; the secret coordinate system comes from you. A private geometric key — your own change of basis — defines the coordinates in which your model actually works.

Step 1

You generate a private geometric key

  • You run a setup routine (or the runtime) that embeds your documents or signals into the λ-geometry space.
  • You generate a private geometric key on your side: in practice, a secret change of coordinates for the model and your data.
  • The system produces a transformed model and transformed data that live entirely in this disguised coordinate system.
Step 2

Training happens in the disguised coordinates

  • Training runs either on:
    • your own GPU/on-prem server, or
    • GPUs you rent in your own cloud account, using the λ-secure runtime.
  • Only the transformed model and transformed data ever touch that compute environment.
  • Neither Logarchéon nor the cloud provider sees plaintext data or a canonical tuned model.
Step 3

Inference stays vendor-blind

  • You can keep everything in the disguised coordinates for operations, or reverse the geometric key locally if you need canonical outputs.
  • Even if someone copies the runtime or model, they obtain only a transformed version, not a directly usable twin.
  • Standard crypto (disk encryption, TLS, TEEs) sits underneath the λ-geometry layer as defense-in-depth.

Plain English You get a self-service “encrypted AI laundromat”: your data and model go in wearing your own geometric disguise; all training and inference happen in that disguise; only you can remove it—and only if your policy says you should.

Security posture (Q&A)

Logarchéon is built for environments where “trust our logs” is not a security model. The posture below states: (i) what is protected, (ii) what is explicitly not claimed, and (iii) what operational controls must hold for the claim to remain true.

Anchor principle: a transformed representation may preserve internal structure for authorized computation, but structure becomes intelligence only when an attacker also has anchors (metadata / identities / join keys) or an operational channel (a way to generate compatible queries or validate hypotheses).

Threat model assumed in this section

Zero-Oracle Exfiltration. An attacker steals only transformed artifacts from storage (e.g., transformed vectors and/or transformed weights), but has no:

  • plaintext inputs or plaintext outputs,
  • before/after pairs,
  • document IDs, labels, timestamps, author fields, case/matter IDs, patient IDs, asset IDs, or other metadata,
  • query interface (no ability to submit inputs and observe outputs),
  • side channels that provide validation.

Plain English Offline read-only possession of transformed numbers, with no way to test guesses against the real system.

Comparison: what changes the risk picture

Attacker also has… What becomes possible Why it matters
Nothing extra (transformed artifacts only) Unsupervised structure may be computed (neighbors/clusters/outliers) but remains unlabeled and non-operational. They cannot reliably tie structure to real clients, events, systems, or identities.
Anchors (IDs, timestamps, labels, provenance, join keys) Structure can be mapped to named entities and time windows; cluster narratives become testable. Anchors convert geometry into intelligence (who/when/where).
Operational channel (query path, endpoint leakage, oracle) Retrieval and hypothesis testing become practical; a stolen index can become actionable. Operational access is the difference between “artifact theft” and “working exploitation.”
Both anchors + operational channel Full exploitation becomes plausible, depending on key hygiene and containment. This is where controls (tenant keys, logging policy, endpoint hardening) decide outcomes.

This table is deliberately explicit: Logarchéon’s strongest posture is against artifact-only theft. If you expand the threat model to include anchors or a query path, the mitigations shift toward operational governance.

Security Q&A

Q: If an attacker steals the full transformed index, what do they get?
They do not get readable plaintext, and they do not get a usable interface to answer real questions the way your users do. They may compute internal numerical relations (e.g., “A is close to B” under the preserved structure), but without anchors and without a compatible query path those relations are not reliably convertible into client-identifiable or operationally actionable intelligence.
Q: Are you claiming the transformed space is “meaningless”?
No. A keyed transformation can preserve specific internal relations so authorized training/inference still works. The claim is narrower and stronger: without keys plus anchoring metadata and/or an operational channel, an attacker cannot reliably convert preserved relations into named, time-bounded, decision-grade intelligence.
Q: Do similar items stay similar?
For authorized computation running inside the keyed runtime: yes—by design, certain relations are preserved so the system remains functional. For an unauthorized observer with only a stolen dump: “similarity exists” does not imply “exploitation is practical,” because they lack anchoring and validation.
Q: Why isn’t clustering (or outliers) automatically a breach?
Clustering and outlier scoring become intelligence only when you can interpret them and test the interpretation. Under the exfiltration-only model, the attacker cannot answer basic questions like “which cluster is which client,” “which outlier is a real incident,” or “what changed and when,” because the needed anchors and verification channels are absent.
Q: Can a stolen transformed index be used as a retrieval engine?
Retrieval requires a query vector in the same keyed coordinate system. Under the assumed model, the attacker has no way to generate compatible transformed queries and no oracle to validate guesses. “Index theft becomes retrieval” is therefore a different threat model—one that requires endpoint compromise, insider access, or query leakage.
Q: What does Logarchéon explicitly not claim?
It does not claim universal “semantic secrecy” against attackers who also possess metadata, partial labels, or external join keys. It does not claim security against a live oracle (query access) under the same assumptions as pure exfiltration. It does not claim complexity-theory “hardness” as a substitute for operational key hygiene.
Q: What operational controls are required for the posture to hold?
Tenant-unique keys and key rotation; strict containment of embeddings/hidden states (no debug dumps, no analytics sinks by default); metadata minimization and segregation; and endpoint hardening (treat query-generation surfaces like credential-bearing systems). These controls prevent anchors and operational channels from accidentally appearing.

MECE view: attacker goals vs. what they need

Goal class A

Label groups (“matters”, “cohorts”, “campaigns”)

  • They can compute: unlabeled clusters.
  • They still need: IDs/timestamps/provenance to attach meaning and validate.
  • Posture: clusters alone do not identify clients, events, or systems.
Goal class B

Prioritize targets (“hot docs”, “urgent incidents”)

  • They can compute: numerical outliers and graph “bridges.”
  • They still need: context to distinguish importance from artifacts.
  • Posture: they can rank weirdness, not mission significance.
Goal class C

Link entities (same author/entity/workflow)

  • They can compute: near-duplicates and similarity graphs.
  • They still need: attribution metadata (who/when/what) to make it actionable.
  • Posture: similarity without attribution is not operational intelligence.
Goal class D

Use the corpus for retrieval

  • They can compute: nearest neighbors for a given query vector.
  • They still need: a compatible query path under the same key (or an oracle).
  • Posture: retrieval becomes relevant only if endpoints leak queries.
Goal class E

Detect change over time (“something happened”)

  • They can compute: drift between dumps.
  • They still need: timestamps and provenance to attribute drift to real events.
  • Posture: change without direction is not usable intelligence.
Key takeaway

What turns structure into intelligence

  • Anchors: IDs, timestamps, labels, provenance, join keys.
  • Operational channel: query access, endpoint leakage, or any oracle.
  • Controls: keys per tenant, rotation, no embedding export by default, metadata containment, hardened endpoints.

Under the hood: CEAS, finite lift, GRAIL, and MIA

The landing page is simple on purpose. Underneath, the stack draws on original work in geometry, symbolic dynamics, and secure computation. The emphasis is: interpretable dynamics, encrypted-in-use execution, and invariant-first hardware.

Core pillars
  • CEAS: critical-entropy attention scaling; treats β as a controlled parameter to cut training steps and improve stability, especially in long-sequence regimes.
  • Finite-machine lift: decomposes model behavior into cycles and transients (Dunford D+N / PDN) for traceability and safe edit-without-full-retrain.
  • GRAIL: geometry-native secure execution aimed at cryptomorphic (function-identical, weight-distinct) twins and encrypted-in-use computation.
  • MIA: metric-invariant architecture where inputs, machine state, and outputs move together under group action; suitable for FPGAs and mature-node silicon.
