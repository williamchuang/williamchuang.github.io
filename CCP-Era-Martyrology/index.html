Catholics Who Died or Are Held Under the Chinese Communist Party: A Calendar, and a Roll of the Imprisoned

A martyrology in calendar order, January to December, of Catholic bishops, priests, religious, and laity who died at the hands of the Chinese Communist Party — before 1949 in areas it controlled, and after 1949 in the People's Republic — together with those who died in its custody or under its confinement, and those who, having refused to compromise, are held by it still. One calendar holds all of them: the dead on the day of death, the detained on the day the present detention began. After the calendar, a roll lists every Catholic known from the public record to be in detention, disappeared, or confined in the People's Republic or Hong Kong as of 12 September 2026, so that no one held is left off because the date of arrest was never disclosed. Each entry carries its canonical status and an evidence grade.

Reading note

The Church has recognized as martyrs none of the persons listed below except where a cause is expressly noted. "Martyr" here is the word the faithful who buried them used, and the page records it as their usage, not as a canonical finding. Every date is the dies natalis, the day of death, which is what a martyrology memorizes; where the state has never disclosed the date, the entry sits in the final section, "Date withheld or unknown," and the memorial is kept on the day the death was confirmed.

00

Scope and Method

Who is included. Three groups, in one calendar. First, Catholics of any nationality who died in territory controlled by the Chinese Communist Party — from the Jiangxi and northern base areas of the 1930s and 1940s through the People's Republic to the present — where the death was caused by Party or state action: execution, death in prison or labor camp, death on a forced march, death under torture, or death in undisclosed custody. Second, those who died at home under house arrest or continuous surveillance after long imprisonment; their entries say so, since the distinction matters for any future cause. Third, the living: bishops and priests who refused to join the state-run Patriotic Association or otherwise to compromise, and who are held in secret detention, forcibly disappeared, or confined under house arrest as of 12 September 2026. The living are entered on the day their present detention or disappearance began, so that the calendar can be prayed as a whole; Section 03 repeats all of them in one roll, adding those for whom no arrest date is public. Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan are in scope; Hong Kong enters with the imprisonment of Jimmy Lai.

What each entry carries. Name in the Western and Chinese forms the person used; office and religious institute; date and place of death; the persecution context; the canonical status of the person's cause; the evidence grade; and sources. Three status labels are used: Servant of God where a cause has been opened; No cause on record where none has been found; Fate unconfirmed for the disappeared; and Detained · refused compromise for the living. Evidence chips follow the house convention: measured date and place documented by more than one independent source; reconstructed assembled from testimony or a single source; unknown withheld by the state.

What the calendar can and cannot hold. Every entry in the calendar is verified against at least one primary or scholarly source. The number of Catholics who died under the Party runs to the thousands; most are known, if at all, only in diocesan memory and in three collections — Fazzini's Red Book of Chinese Martyrs, Moreau's Blood of the Martyrs, and Clark's China's Modern Martyrs series. Section 05 records, by persecution and diocese, the persons whose arrest or death is documented but whose day of death the sources do not give; they belong to the martyrology as much as those with dates, and are listed there so that the record of their names is not lost for want of a day.

No claim is made on this page that any person listed has been recognized by the Church as a martyr unless a cause is expressly cited. The word "martyr" in the names of collections, feasts, and popular usage is reported as such. Statements about the manner of death rest on the sources cited for each entry and are graded accordingly.

Bishop Joseph Fan Zhongliang, S.J. 范忠良
Underground bishop of Shanghai · b. 18 December 1918 · ordained 1951 by Bishop Ignatius Kung Pin-mei · arrested in the Shanghai round-up of 8 September 1955; about twenty years in labor camps in Qinghai · consecrated clandestinely 27 February 1985 · under house arrest from 2000 to his death at 95 · died under confinement, not in custody

April
13 April 1992 — Baoding (Hebei), in custody; body left at the family's door 16 April
Bishop Peter Joseph Fan Xueyan 范學淹
Underground bishop of Baoding · b. 29 December 1907 · consecrated 24 June 1951 · imprisoned 1958–1979 and repeatedly thereafter · detained without warrant from late 1990 · died at 84

Held at the former bishop's residence in Baoding without warrant or trial for about eighteen months; his body was returned to relatives wrapped in plastic, with marks around the neck and bruising to the chest, forehead, and legs. Bishop Fan was the principal architect of the underground hierarchy in Hebei, having consecrated bishops clandestinely with faculties from Rome. The Holy See was not notified of his death by the state; the family and the Associated Press reported it.[2, 3, 5]

Coadjutor bishop of Xuanhua, recognized by Rome, not by the state · b. 1950 · persecuted since 1993; most of the intervening years spent in detention or house arrest · has refused to join the Patriotic Association · held in secret, indefinite detention without charge; family visits intermittently allowed · under detention or house arrest as of April 2026

Bishop · arrested in June 1966, at the opening of the Cultural Revolution, on charges of counter-revolutionary activity and relations with foreigners · died in prison as a consequence of severe mistreatment

Fr. Sergio Ticozzi's 2016 survey in Tripod, as summarized by World Mission and by Human Rights Without Frontiers, gives the month and the cause but not the diocese or the day. Also in June, for the record of the calendar: on the night of 15 June 1953 Fr. Zhu Shude, S.J., was arrested in Shanghai — the beginning of the thirty years that ended on 28 December 1983 (see December).[21]

1, 10, and 23 July 1947 — Yangjiaping (Chahar, now Hebei): the three "People's Courts" of the Trappist community
The Trappists of Our Lady of Consolation, Yangjiaping — the trials 楊家坪聖母神慰院
Cistercians of the Strict Observance (O.C.S.O.) · Chinese and French monks · abbey surrounded by Communist forces in 1939, pillaged spring 1947, tried before organized village assemblies on 1, 10, and 23 July 1947, community chained and imprisoned in its own refectory

August
26 August 1961 — Baihu labor farm (Lujiang, Anhui): starved to death on the boat carrying him to the farm hospital
Fr. Matthew Chen Zhemin 陳哲敏（陳文中）
Diocesan priest, b. 1909, Quxian, Sichuan · doctorate in philosophy, Urbaniana, Rome, where he taught · Chinese secretary to the first internuncio, Archbishop Antonio Riberi, 1946 · co-founder of the Catholic Central Bureau, 1947 · professor of ethics and philosophy at Aurora College for Women, 1948, where he told the students that if you give up one step on a staircase you must climb two to regain it · one of the "four talents" of the Chinese Church of the 1950s (董世祉, 張伯達, 陳哲敏, 沈士賢) · arrested 6 September 1951 at the Central Bureau with Frs. Shen Shixian, Hou Zhizheng, Le Grand, and Prevost · sentenced 1960 to twenty years as one of three priests who "showed no repentance" · hard labor at Huangshan and the Digang quarry; transferred 16 August 1961 to the heavy-labor brigade at Baihu · died ten days later of starvation and overwork, anointed by Fr. Fu Hezhou · buried on Majiashan hill by the Baihu hospital
No cause on recordmeasured

The first of the Shanghai "four talents" to die in custody; Fr. Beda Chang (張伯達) had died in 1951 (see 11 November). When told the Legion of Mary was the cause of the trouble, he answered that without the Legion they would have found the Sodality, or the Eucharistic Crusade, or any other group — the target was the whole Church.[28, 31]

30 August 1947 — Yangjiaping: the abbey burned; the death march begins
The Trappists of Our Lady of Consolation, Yangjiaping — the thirty-three 楊家坪聖母神慰院殉道者
O.C.S.O. · thirty-three monks and associated faithful died between the burning of the abbey on 30 August 1947 and 1948, on a forced march through the mountains and in Communist custody, among them Fr. Guillaume Cambourieu (1870–1947), Fr. Seraphin Shi (1909–1948), and Br. Alexis Liu (d. 1948) · the death toll rose from sixteen known in 1947 to thirty-three
No cause on recordreconstructed

The largest single Catholic death event under the Party before 1949, and the one Chinese Catholics still speak of quietly. The abbey, founded in 1883, was the mother house of Our Lady of Joy (Liesse), later re-founded on Lantau Island, Hong Kong. Individual dates of death for the thirty-three are recorded in Jen and in Moreau; this entry keeps the memorial on the day the abbey fell.[7, 8, 9, 10]

29 October 2025 — Zhengding (Hebei), under detention or house arrest that had run since 2020
Bishop Julius Jia Zhiguo 賈治國
Underground bishop of Zhengding · b. 1 May 1935 · first imprisoned 1963 for refusing to renounce the Pope · ordained priest 7 June 1980 and bishop 19 December 1980 by Bishop Fan Xueyan · never joined the Patriotic Association · arrested more than a dozen times; under house arrest from 2018, moved by police to a hotel in 2020 · his orphanage for disabled children, run for thirty years with religious sisters, dismantled by police · the state acknowledged that his illness was largely a product of his detentions · died at 90

8 November 2015 — found dead in the shallows of the Fen River near Taiyuan (Shanxi)
Fr. Pedro Yu Heping 余和平
Priest of the underground community, aged 41 · energetic and, by every account of those who knew him, never depressed · found dead after what ecclesial sources following the case describe as rough interrogation · police classified the death as suicide within days; no one in the Chinese Church who knew him accepts the verdict

11 November 1951 — Shanghai, Lokawei prison; body returned 12 November
Fr. Beda Chang, S.J. 張伯達
Society of Jesus · b. c. 1905 as Zhang Zhengming · principal of St. Ignatius College and head of the Bureau of Chinese Studies, Zikawei · arrested 9 August 1951 for refusing to lead Shanghai's Catholics into the state-directed "reform" movement · died in custody at about 46
No cause on recordmeasured

Fr. Aedan McGrath, in the cell opposite, saw him fail over two months before he died on the morning of 11 November. Shanghai's Catholics turned out in such numbers for the requiem Masses that the government denounced the prayers as "a new type of bacteria warfare." He is the first priest to die in Communist custody after 1949 and remains the Shanghai Church's own protomartyr in popular memory.[11, 12]

Fr. Wang Rensheng, S.J. 王仁生
Society of Jesus, Shanghai · with Fr. 28 December 1983 — Hefei (Anhui), municipal prison
Fr. Zhu Shude, S.J. 朱樹德
Society of Jesus · b. 21 December 1913 · ordained 19 May 1945 · doctorate in geography, Paris, 1949 · returned from Hong Kong to Shanghai on 19 October 1949 by order of the Jesuit General, against every entreaty to stay — "in the eyes of the world I am indeed the biggest fool ever born" · chaplain to Aurora University Catholics, then pastor of Christ the King · arrested the night of 15 June 1953 · sentenced 17 March 1960 to twenty years; kept at Baihu farm, Anhui, after the term expired · rearrested November 1981 and sentenced to twelve more · died in prison at 70, one week after his birthday
No cause on recordmeasured

Three of his brothers were priests. Fu Jen Catholic University in Taiwan named its law building 樹德樓 for him in 1999, and his tombstone stands in the Jesuit cemetery at Ching Shan, Changhua. "As long as there are Christians left in Shanghai, I must return there. Because I am a priest. Wherever I am, the Church is."[21, 22]

30 December 2009 — Xiwanzi (Hebei); news of the death controlled by the authorities
Bishop Leo Yao Liang 姚良
Underground auxiliary bishop of Xiwanzi · b. 1923, Gonghui village, Zhangbei · ordained 1946 · sent to labor camp 1956 and sentenced to life in 1958 for communion with the Pope; released 1984 after twenty-eight years · consecrated bishop 19 February 2002 · arrested again 31 March 2005 and 30 July 2006 and held at Zhangjiakou · died at 87
No cause on recordreconstructed

Whether he was in detention or under house arrest on the day he died is not established from the sources available; the suppression of the news of his death is. Three priests of the neighboring diocese of Xuanhua — Frs. Zhang Cunhui, Zhang Zhanglin, and a Fr. Liu — had disappeared into custody the previous June.[23]

What the roll shows
Eight bishops, at least six priests, and one layman are on the public record as held, disappeared, or confined as of 12 September 2026. Every bishop on the roll is one the Holy See recognizes; every one is held for the same reason, refusal of the Patriotic Association or of the state's control of the episcopate; and not one has been charged in open court except the layman in Hong Kong. The roll's shortness is a measure of secrecy, not of the persecution.

The asymmetry is the finding. Of the persons the Chinese Church itself names first when it names its modern martyrs, only the one American bishop has a cause, and that cause was opened in the United States. No cause can be opened inside the People's Republic, and the diocesan phase of a cause requires the competent bishop, the documents, and the witnesses to be available to a tribunal. The withholding of bodies, dates, and records recorded in Section 02 is, among other things, the mechanism by which that door is kept shut.

05

Recorded Without a Day: The Persecutions by Diocese

The persons below are documented as arrested, imprisoned, or killed for the faith under the Party, but the sources cited do not give a day of death, or the person survived imprisonment and died later in freedom. They are set down by persecution and diocese so that the names stand with the calendar. Names already carrying a calendar entry are marked with the date; the rest carry what the record gives.
