Ramanujan's Work Algorithms

An exhaustive reverse-engineering of Srinivasa Ramanujan's methods, restricted to pre-1920 mathematics. Thirty-three reconstructed strategies across five problem categories, traced against eighty sampled formulae from the notebooks and lost notebook. Verified, updated, then extended to modern equivalent starting points with three worked demonstrations. A separate 50-node worldwide frontier tree covers the Hardy–Littlewood approximation lineage through Vinogradov, Bourgain, Écalle, Keating–Snaith, and Kelley–Meka — and, added in the second pass, motivic periods and the cosmic Galois group (Brown), Feynman integral relations (Broadhurst–Roberts), arithmetic quantum field theory (Harvard CMSA 2024), the prime geodesic theorem for large genus (Wu–Xue 2022), higher Teichmüller & Hitchin components (Pollicott–Sharp), and SL(2,ℝ) orbit-closure rigidity (Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi) — twelve branches in total, placing each within the Langlands programme.

05 The Approximation Methods Tree

Techniques Ramanujan would have learned or encountered through Hardy, Littlewood, and the broader international analytic tradition — extended to the worldwide frontier as of 2026. Fifty methods across twelve branches, spanning Stokes (1850) through Kelley–Meka (2023), with two branches added in the second pass: H11 (Motivic Periods & Feynman Amplitudes — cosmic Galois group, multiple zeta values, Bessel moments, mixed L-functions) and H12 (Teichmüller Dynamics & Spectral Counting — prime geodesic theorem for large genus, Hitchin components, Mirzakhani volume recursion, SL(2,ℝ) orbit closures). School attribution and frontier status are marked for each.

Selection criterion: top-tier, globally competitive practice. No school — Cambridge, Princeton, Paris, Moscow, Beijing, Tokyo, Seoul, Tel Aviv — is privileged. The criterion is the strongest known result for each problem class.
