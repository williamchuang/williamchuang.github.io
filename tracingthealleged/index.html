Tracing the Alleged Chi Haotian Speech

From Hillsdale to Boxun: provenance, authorship, intellectual genealogy, and policy transmission

Working research record  ·  July 31, 2026  ·  Logarchéon

Research trigger and problem

This inquiry began after I watched Hillsdale College’s newly posted video China’s Most Dangerous Weapon Can Fit in Your Pocket on July 30, 2026. The video republishes a March 7, 2023 lecture in which Brian T. Kennedy, chairman of the Committee on the Present Danger: China, treats an alleged secret speech attributed to former Chinese defense minister Chi Haotian as a central source for a claimed Chinese biological-war strategy against the United States.

That connection creates a plausible policy-transmission route whose official use requires internal briefing or decision records.

A second trigger was the recurrence of the same chronological anchor in Gordon G. Chang’s public commentary. Chang and Kennedy both place unusual weight on May 2019 and the phrase “People’s War.” At the outset, however, that recurrence was treated as a set of unanswered questions rather than as corroboration: how widely had the interpretation circulated; was it being used as the starting point for a broader theory of conflict; what did the underlying May 2019 text itself establish; and had the speakers reached the interpretation independently, inherited it through a shared public source chain, or received common briefings? Each question could supply a sufficient reason, a necessary reason, or both, for undertaking this study. Section 01 states the decision rule; Section 07 returns an answer to every question.

“I want to find the true author and original source of this article: blog.creaders.net/u/460/202002/366547.html. Who was the first person on the Internet to express the idea—even if the wording differs— ‘如果生物武器偷袭成功，中国人民将在对美斗争中付出最小牺牲代价’?”

Those findings make him an upstream origin-of-information subject; authorship remains a separate provenance question.

Research Problem, Scope, and Evidentiary Design

The inquiry separates political significance from documentary authenticity and treats influence as a reason to investigate while reserving proof for provenance.

Kennedy treats the alleged speech as a central source. After introducing Unrestricted Warfare, he devotes a substantial part of the lecture to quoting and interpreting the text as evidence of a Chinese plan to depopulate and occupy the United States. Within that argument, the anonymous document functions as a principal source. Its renewed prominence creates a legitimate International Relations and International Law research problem: how can an unauthenticated text acquire sufficient institutional authority to shape threat perception and potentially enter policy discourse?

The research objective is narrower than proving or disproving every claim about China. It is to identify the document’s earliest recoverable form, distinguish authorship from transmission, reconstruct its intellectual inputs, test the Chi Haotian attribution, and identify how later interpreters converted repetition into the appearance of corroboration.

Analytic symmetry and anti-capture rule

This journal treats PRC united-front narratives, exile-media narratives, Trump-aligned policy advocacy, anti-Masonic claims, public-health institutional accounts, and official denials as interested source environments rather than default frames. A source may accurately preserve a document while still selecting, amplifying, or interpreting it in ways that serve institutional interests. The analysis therefore states what each public record affirmatively establishes, identifies the additional insider or archival evidence needed for stronger conclusions, and keeps capability, intent, authorship, coordination, and implementation as separate questions.

Unanswered justification questions at the beginning of the inquiry

The study did not begin by assuming that repetition proved either authenticity or coordination. It began by asking whether four distinct propositions could justify the research. A question is sufficient when an affirmative answer alone warrants a disciplined inquiry; it is necessary when the larger evidentiary or policy judgment cannot responsibly be made without answering it; and it is both when it independently justifies the study and must also be resolved before the recurring narrative can carry its claimed weight.

Why He Xin entered the inquiry

He Xin entered the inquiry through backward source tracing rather than prior selection as a suspect. The search moved from the exact 2005 sentence to earlier formulations of the same underlying idea. His March 2002 interview supplies the 20/80 depopulation framework and the use of war, epidemics, biological weapons, and genetic technology. His 2003 essay, later republished as 《中国古代生物战争的起源及社会与文化后果》, goes further than a generic warning: it reconstructs an alleged Xiongnu use of infected horses, livestock, animal remains, roads, and water sources against Han forces, calls the episode an early form of “生化武器”, and expressly argues that the Chinese state should elevate biochemical warfare, as a form of contemporary asymmetric warfare, to strategic study. The anonymous 2005 text then cites He Xin directly and reverses his public victim-threat narrative into an offensive Chinese first-strike proposition.

Attribution consequence. This discovery places He Xin's corpus beyond the role of a general thematic background source. It makes direct borrowing, follower or assistant adaptation, and possible authorial participation more serious hypotheses. The anonymous-compiler hypothesis, an associated-writer hypothesis, and direct participation by He Xin all remain live pending provenance evidence.
