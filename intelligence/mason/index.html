Reconstruction method

The unit of analysis is a public-source decision unit: situation, uncertainty, why-question ladder, Mason-style work move, likely artifact, skill set, and caution. It is not mind-reading. It is a historically constrained reconstruction of how an officer who connected foreign observation, library records, hydrography, language skill, and bureau service might organize naval intelligence work.

Core thesis

Mason's method was the birth logic of permanent American naval intelligence: collect foreign naval information before crisis, record it systematically, translate it into bureau-useful answers, and preserve the result as institutional memory.

Case unit

Each row asks what a Mason-style officer would likely ask first: authority, source, index heading, bureau user, technical implication, geographic constraint, modernization value, and archival residue.

Ethical reading

The page emphasizes lawful observation, diplomatic restraint, provenance, uncertainty, and civilian-control records. It treats intelligence as disciplined professional knowledge, not romantic secrecy.

4

4

Limits, ethics, and use

Not a tradecraft page

This page is for historical and institutional analysis. It does not teach clandestine methods, recruitment, sabotage, evasion, or modern collection procedures.

Founder narrative caution

Mason's role as founder and first head is well attested in public profiles, but the deeper day-to-day texture of the first office should be checked against primary Navy records before academic publication.

Modern boundary

Modern ONI is a large maritime intelligence organization; this page deliberately stops at Mason-era origin logic and does not infer current capabilities or procedures from 1880s sources.
