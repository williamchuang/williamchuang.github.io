Reconstruction method

The unit of analysis is a public-source decision unit, not a claim that 300 discrete missions are documented. Each row asks: if we are reading James’s case as an intelligence-history problem, what situation starts the question, what would a careful interpreter ask, what decision logic follows, which skill is central, and what ethical or evidentiary guardrail must remain visible?

Core thesis

James Lafayette’s intelligence value came from the intersection of local Virginia knowledge, coerced social status, British assumptions, Lafayette’s command need, and the dangerous discipline of carrying useful information to the Patriot side. His story is therefore both an intelligence success and an indictment of the republic’s unfinished liberty.

Case unit

Each case has a family, situation, question ladder, action logic, likely artifact, skill, strategy tags, and a guardrail. The emphasis is source criticism and historical judgment rather than operational imitation.

Ethical reading

The page honors courage without sanitizing bondage. Every success frame is paired with the fact that James returned to enslavement after the war and had to petition for freedom and later pension recognition.

01

Decision tree: reading James Lafayette as method

1. Start with status and constraint

Identify the coercive conditions of enslavement before describing choice, access, movement, or reward.

2. Define the command question

Ask what Lafayette needed to know about British movement, intention, reinforcement, headquarters posture, or Yorktown plans.

3. Separate channels

Keep British-facing deception, Patriot-facing reporting, later petitions, and modern commemoration in distinct lanes.

4. Validate the claim

Rank evidence: petition, Lafayette testimonial, legislative action, biography, museum summary, later portrait, and public memory.

5. Connect to decision, not myth

Explain how intelligence could support allied decisions while avoiding “one person single-handedly won Yorktown” overstatement.

6. Preserve the contradiction

End every achievement story with the postwar reality: freedom had to be requested, proven, legislated, and later recognized only partially.

Limits and ethical commitments

No tradecraft manual

The page does not teach clandestine procedure, cover creation, deception tactics, message handling, evasion, or recruitment. It discusses those topics only as historical evidence, risk, and ethics.

Naming precision

The header uses the common modern name “James Armistead Lafayette” for recognition. Body copy explains that contemporary records often identify him as James, James Fayette, or James Lafayette.

Archive humility

The record is incomplete and shaped by slavery, law, memory, and later commemoration. The page marks uncertainty rather than forcing false precision.
