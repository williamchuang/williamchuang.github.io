Reconstruction method

The unit of analysis is not “what secret instruction did Xu give?” It is a public-source decision unit: situation, uncertainty, why-question ladder, likely institutional move, skill family, and guardrail. The page follows the visual and structural logic of the Casey / Dulles / Donovan templates while shifting the analytic axis to Republican China, party-state intelligence, and source criticism.

Core thesis

Xu’s recurring method can be read as a fusion of engineering-bureaucratic habits, radio-administrative access, CC Clique patronage, party-organization files, civilian intelligence networks, and anti-Communist security priorities. The same structure created reach, but also catastrophic trust failures, factional capture, corruption exposure, and legitimacy damage.

Case unit

Each row asks what Xu’s system would have had to clarify: authority, file, person, channel, faction, rival service, source reliability, coercive risk, future archive, and the institutional cost of being wrong.

Ethical reading

Successes and failures are both evidence. The page treats coercive party-security practices, mole failures, corruption allegations, and factional rivalry as cautionary governance studies rather than examples to imitate.

Limits and ethics

Not a tradecraft manual

This page deliberately avoids actionable intelligence procedures. It abstracts historical cases into governance questions: authority, source reliability, oversight, legality, corruption, and institutional legitimacy.

Contested memory

Xu’s world is source-difficult: memoir, hostile accounts, party history, popular legend, and fragmentary institutional records do not always agree. The correct method is triangulation, not certainty theater.

Modern transfer rule

Only governance lessons transfer: trust audits, routing redundancy, file accuracy, anti-corruption controls, legal authority checks, source criticism, and institutional humility.
