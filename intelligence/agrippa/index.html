Reconstruction method

The unit is a public-source decision-analysis unit, not mind-reading and not magical instruction. Each row converts a historical problem into a sequence: situation, diagnostic questions, action logic, skills, and strategy tags. The method follows the Logarchéon 33-strategy / 300-case template while adapting the content to Renaissance humanism, theology, law, medicine, print culture, and reception history.

Core thesis

Agrippa’s method is best read as disciplined synthesis under risk: compile authorities, arrange them into a symbolic architecture, critique the vanity of disciplines, defend vulnerable persons through law and medicine, and survive patronage, censorship, and legend.

Case unit

Each case asks what Agrippa would likely distinguish first: source status, discipline boundary, theological limit, patronage risk, rhetorical frame, and later reception.

Ethical overlay

The page treats occult philosophy as intellectual history and symbolic systems analysis. It does not present operative recipes; controversial materials are reframed as claims, metaphors, texts, and reception artifacts.

Decision tree: reading Agrippa as method

1. What kind of claim is this?

Historical fact, source quotation, Agrippan doctrine, theological boundary, legal argument, medical explanation, hostile legend, or modern analogy.

2. Which discipline owns the first distinction?

Assign the problem to philology, natural philosophy, theology, law, medicine, rhetoric, patronage, print history, or reception before arguing.

3. What source chain supports it?

Map classical, biblical, scholastic, Hermetic, Cabalistic, legal, medical, or printed witnesses and identify inherited distortions.

4. Where is the boundary?

Mark the line between description and practice, nature and superstition, symbol and cause, critique and nihilism, rhetoric and sincere thesis.

5. Who is the hostile reader?

Imagine the censor, inquisitor, rival scholar, demonologist, modern sensationalist, or careless practitioner who will misuse the passage.

6. What artifact should survive?

Leave a concordance, distinction table, claim-status ledger, source spine, reception map, or ethical note for later reconstruction.

Complete question atlas by situation type

These are the recurring question sets for reading Agrippa historically, philosophically, and ethically.

Humanist textual quarry

  1. Which authority is being invoked?
  2. Is the claim primary, translated, excerpted, or inherited?
  3. What contradiction must be preserved?
  4. What index would let the reader verify it?
  5. Where does admiration become credulity?

Occult philosophy architecture

  1. Which world—elemental, celestial, or intellectual—does the claim occupy?
  2. What mediates between levels?
  3. What is a correspondence rather than a cause?
  4. Where is the Christian boundary placed?
  5. What would a hostile theologian attack?

Natural magic boundary

  1. Can the effect be framed through hidden natural virtues?
  2. What separates natural inquiry from superstition?
  3. What evidence is textual, empirical, symbolic, or devotional?
  4. Who judges licitness?
  5. What safer formulation preserves the question?

Skeptical critique

  1. What does this art claim to know?
  2. Where does its practice contradict its theory?
  3. What moral vanity attaches to its experts?
  4. What remains after critique?
  5. Does the critique destroy or purify inquiry?

Gender polemic

  1. Which misogynist premise is being reversed?
  2. Which scriptural or classical authority supports the reversal?
  3. Is the praise sincere, rhetorical, or both?
  4. What dignity claim survives the genre?
  5. How would modern gender history read the move?

Witchcraft accusation

  1. What evidence exists beyond rumor and fear?
  2. Could law or medicine explain the case better than demonology?
  3. Who is vulnerable to coercion?
  4. What procedure protects the accused?
  5. What does the case reveal about learned authority?

Patronage and office

  1. Who can protect the work or role?
  2. What does the patron want?
  3. Which office creates duties of speech or silence?
  4. What happens when intellectual independence conflicts with service?
  5. What record should survive patronage politics?

Publication and censorship

  1. Which passage will be weaponized by enemies?
  2. What preface, dedication, or caveat should frame the text?
  3. Is the manuscript ready for print?
  4. Which edition will future readers inherit?
  5. What source spine makes the work defensible?

Reception and myth

  1. What belongs to Agrippa’s own works?
  2. What belongs to hostile anecdote or demonological legend?
  3. How did later readers reuse him?
  4. What does the Faustian image distort?
  5. Why does the myth remain culturally useful?

Translation and editions

  1. Which witness is being used?
