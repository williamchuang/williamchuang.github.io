Reconstruction method

The unit of analysis is not a secret instruction. It is a historically bounded decision unit: situation, diagnostic why-questions, likely public-office move, artifact, skill set, and guardrail. The page uses the same Logarchéon 33-method / 300-case format as the Casey, Dulles, and Donovan pages, but Bonaparte’s case is a reform-law and institutional-birth problem rather than an intelligence-operations problem.

Core thesis

Bonaparte’s recurring method was to convert public disgust into lawful administrative machinery: merit systems instead of patronage, files instead of rumor, prosecutable cases instead of slogans, and bounded investigative capacity instead of borrowed or informal detective work.

Case unit

Each row asks what Bonaparte would likely ask first: authority, public duty, evidence, appointment integrity, federal jurisdiction, rights impact, presidential pressure, departmental record, and future legitimacy.

Ethical reading

The strongest lesson is double-edged. The same reform instinct that made a federal investigative force useful also requires a legitimacy architecture: boundaries, records, supervision, congressional sensitivity, and civil-liberties caution.

Decision tree: reading Bonaparte as method

1. Identify the public wrong

Is the problem patronage, machine control, corporate concentration, departmental incompetence, unequal citizenship, or investigative incapacity?

2. Locate the authority

Find the city rule, civil-service standard, statute, departmental power, presidential direction, or federal jurisdictional basis.

3. Demand evidence before rhetoric

Convert accusation into documents, files, names, dates, contracts, voting barriers, market facts, or departmental records.

4. Convert principle into machinery

A reform slogan must become an examination, file system, prosecution, inspection, order, memo, or durable office.

5. Audit legitimacy

Ask who is protected, who is burdened, what rights are at stake, and whether Congress or the public would see lawful reform or administrative evasion.

6. Preserve the record

Write the decision so a successor, court, historian, or citizen can reconstruct what power was used and why.

Question atlas — 12 situation families

These are the reusable question sets. The 300 corpus rows below instantiate them across Bonaparte’s reform, cabinet, legal, and institutional legacy domains.

Baltimore machine politics

A local machine environment makes reform a test of evidence, courage, and coalition discipline.

  • What incentive holds the machine together?
  • Which fact can be documented rather than merely alleged?
  • What remedy prevents the abuse from returning?

Civil-service reform

Patronage and appointment practices must be converted into merit rules, fitness tests, and durable oversight.

  • What public function requires demonstrated competence?
  • Which patronage pressure is trying to bypass the rule?
  • Who audits the merit standard after the reformers leave?

National municipal reform

Local reform is translated into national civic doctrine through leagues, speeches, model rules, and networks.

  • What local lesson can be generalized without losing context?
  • Which coalition can carry the reform beyond one city?
  • What model rule turns rhetoric into governance?

Harvard, law, and elite formation

Education, law, and lineage create credibility that must be converted into public obligation rather than entitlement.

  • How does education become public discipline rather than status display?
  • What authority is actually legal rather than social?
  • How does independence remain accountable?

Board of Indian Commissioners

Administrative oversight tests the relation between reform conscience, federal bureaucracy, affected communities, and recordkeeping.

  • Whose testimony is missing from the administrative file?
  • What federal duty is being performed or neglected?
  • How can oversight record harm without claiming more certainty than the facts allow?

Navy Department governance

Departmental leadership requires personnel standards, procurement scrutiny, readiness review, and cabinet-level implementation.

  • Which readiness claim depends on administrative integrity?
  • Where do procurement, personnel, and discipline intersect?
  • What record should survive departmental turnover?

Justice Department authority

The Attorney General must translate law into enforcement priorities, case files, supervision, and institutional limits.

  • What law gives Justice a lane?
  • Which case clarifies federal enforcement priorities?
  • What evidence must exist before public pressure becomes prosecution?

Federal investigative capacity

Justice lacks its own regular investigative force and must design a bounded, supervised, legally defensible capacity.

  • What investigative capacity is missing inside Justice?
  • How can agents be supervised without local machine capture?
  • What boundary prevents a regular force from becoming arbitrary secret power?

Antitrust and corporate power

Corporate concentration is evaluated as a legal, evidentiary, and public-interest problem rather than a slogan.

  • What conduct violates law rather than merely offends reform sentiment?
  • How does private concentration create public consequence?
  • What remedy is proportional and administratively enforceable?

Voting rights and civic equality

Good-government reform is tested by whether it protects Black voting rights, equal citizenship, and local dignity.

  • Whose rights are formally recognized but practically denied?
  • How does reform protect vulnerable citizens rather than only efficient administration?
  • What proof distinguishes discrimination from ordinary political conflict?

Roosevelt reform network

Friendship and shared reform commitments create speed, but must be separated from evidence and lawful responsibility.

  • What does Roosevelt's reform mandate require in this department?
  • Where might personal trust distort institutional judgment?
  • What document converts conversation into accountable action?

Institutional legacy and source record

The later story of Bonaparte depends on archives, origin narratives, accountability, and the balance between myth and evidence.

  • What source object anchors the claim?
  • Where does later institutional mythology exceed the evidence?
  • What lesson should survive without hiding tradeoffs?

Limits & ethics

Not operational guidance

This is a public-source historical and institutional-analysis page. It does not provide investigative tactics, surveillance procedures, confidential-source handling, or modern law-enforcement advice.

Founder myth warning

Bonaparte is useful as an institutional founder, but origin stories can hide later expansions, controversies, and civil-liberties questions. The page therefore treats the Bureau origin as both reform capacity and legitimacy problem.

Public-office standard

The reusable lesson is not “create more power.” It is: define lawful authority, build records, protect rights, discipline personnel, and make public institutions harder to capture by private machines.
