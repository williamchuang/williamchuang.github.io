Reconstruction method

The unit of analysis is not “what secret instruction did Borodin give?” It is a public-source decision unit: situation, starting uncertainty, why-question ladder, institutional move, output artifact, skill family, and caution. The reconstruction is intentionally comparative with the Casey, Dulles, and Donovan templates: 33 overlapping strategy cards, a ranked prevalence map, and a 300-row corpus.

Core thesis

Borodin’s recurring method combined revolutionary-international mandate translation, party constitution drafting, cadre education, coalition management, anti-imperial framing, foreign-aid leverage, political-military architecture, and source-mediated diplomacy. The strength was institution-building speed; the danger was coalition contradiction, external dependency, coercive party-state design, and center-periphery vulnerability.

Reading unit

Each row asks where the problem starts, which Borodin-style diagnostic questions control it, what move would organize action, what document or institution would be produced, and what modern caution must be attached.

Ethical overlay

The page treats propaganda, cadre formation, united-front design, and party-army fusion as historical objects of analysis. It does not convert those techniques into modern instructions.

Decision tree: reading Borodin as method

1. Locate the mandate

Is the situation driven by Moscow, the Comintern, Sun’s government, KMT procedure, military necessity, or later historiography?

2. Name the local constraint

Translate the directive into Canton realities: warlords, merchants, labor, peasants, army commanders, party factions, language, and money.

3. Convert rhetoric into institution

Ask what rule, congress, school, committee, army structure, or program turns ideology into repeatable behavior.

4. Map the coalition contradiction

Identify what the KMT, CCP, Soviet center, army, workers, peasants, and regional commanders want from the same alliance.

5. Audit coercive capacity

Determine who controls arms, officer training, political departments, security, and access to campaign success.

6. Attach a blowback note

Ask how this institutional gain could create dependency, purge risk, authoritarian capacity, or long-memory grievance.

Limits, ethics, and use

Not a manual

This page is not for organizing parties, manipulating publics, running covert political work, or reproducing revolutionary techniques. It is a historical reading instrument for decisions, assumptions, institutions, contradictions, and consequences.

Contested legacy

Borodin helped make the KMT organizationally formidable, but the same institutional machinery intensified united-front contradictions and fed later coercive party-state models. The page preserves that tension.

Archive gaps

Public accounts differ across KMT, CCP, Soviet, Western, memoir, archival, and literary traditions. Every case should be treated as a prompt for verification rather than a final verdict.
