Reconstruction method

The unit is a public-source decision prompt, not a claim to know private instructions. Each case combines a source-family situation, why-question ladder, Faunt-style paper move, skill family, strategy tags, and guardrail.

Core thesis

Faunt's method was secretarial before it was dramatic: receive, sort, abstract, route, draft, carry, and remember. His value lay in trusted paper work inside Walsingham's wider information system.

Case unit

Each row asks what an aide in Faunt's position would need to know: source position, dispatch timing, paper custody, principal intent, correspondence boundary, and archival survival.

Ethical overlay

The reconstruction treats Elizabethan intelligence as a state-paper and decision-history problem. It emphasizes uncertainty, legitimacy, and record limits rather than romanticized spying.

Complete question atlas by situation type

The question families are rewritten as safe historical decision questions. They are designed for reading Faunt's paper world, not for teaching intelligence practice.

Office overload

  1. What business is arriving?
  2. What is urgent, routine, sensitive, or personal?
  3. Which clerk or principal owns the next action?
  4. What record prevents loss?
  5. What can be safely ignored?

Dispatch and courier case

  1. What must move?
  2. How time-sensitive is it?
  3. Who must receive it?
  4. What receipt or reply closes the loop?
  5. What exposure risk travels with it?

Foreign intelligence letter

  1. What did the writer know firsthand?
  2. What is rumor or inference?
  3. What does London need from this?
  4. What corroboration is possible?
  5. What caveat travels with the report?

Walsingham task

  1. What does the principal secretary intend?
  2. Which document performs that intention?
  3. What belongs to Faunt and what returns to Walsingham?
  4. How is accountability preserved?
  5. What future question will the archive ask?

Anthony Bacon channel

  1. Is this friendship, counsel, or office business?
  2. What can Anthony Bacon usefully know?
  3. What should remain bounded?
  4. What religious or political trust is operating?
  5. How might intimacy distort evidence?

Confessional security

  1. What Protestant or Catholic network is being read?
  2. What threat is concrete?
  3. What is atmosphere?
  4. Who benefits if fear is amplified?
  5. What conscience question remains?

Continental travel

  1. Which city, court, church, or university matters?
  2. What did Faunt observe directly?
  3. What comes from conversation?
  4. What can be checked elsewhere?
  5. How does the route become a network map?

Secretaryship theory

  1. What is the office for?
  2. What cumber and variableness obstruct it?
  3. What method and order would help?
  4. Which clerks must be trusted?
  5. What burden should not be hidden?

Signet procedure

  1. What instrument gives force to the action?
  2. What form must be correct?
  3. Who is accountable?
  4. What register preserves regularity?
  5. Where might procedure mask weak policy?

Parliamentary lane

  1. What public role is being performed?
  2. What information flows through representation?
  3. What remains separate from intelligence-adjacent service?
  4. What local or court signal matters?
  5. What is the risk of conflation?

Archive reconstruction

  1. Where did the paper survive?
  2. Is it original, abstract, copy, catalogue, or edition?
  3. What context is missing?
  4. Which source spine confirms it?
  5. What should the page refuse to claim?

Historical safety

  1. Does this teach procedure or analyze decisions?
  2. Can the case be abstracted to evidence, authority, and consequence?
  3. What would be unsafe to operationalize?
  4. What ethics note should be attached?
  5. How is uncertainty shown?

Limits, ethics, and use

Not a manual

The page analyzes early modern records and decisions. It does not provide intelligence tradecraft, cipher instruction, clandestine-contact procedure, or modern operational guidance.

Thin record caution

Faunt is less documented than Walsingham, Dulles, Donovan, or Casey. The page therefore uses source-family prompts and explicit uncertainty rather than pretending to possess a full diary of decisions.

Secretary-centered reading

The focus is the secretary's work: receiving, drafting, abstracting, routing, preserving, and advising. That lens may understate field actors and overstate the legibility of surviving paper.
