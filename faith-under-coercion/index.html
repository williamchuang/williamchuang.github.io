Faith under Coercion
Bridge and Eyes, the Control of Moral Categories, and the Long Tradition of Faith against Communism

Tuitio Fidei et Obsequium Pauperum

Two questions, answered in order. What does a Catholic of Taiwan owe to the Church on both sides of the strait — to a local Church that the universal Church rarely sees, and to a universal Church that Taiwan rarely sees into? And how does a coercive system reach a person — through what categories, records, and pressures — and what, on the evidence of history and of psychology, holds when it does? The first is a mission. The second is an analysis.

William Huanshan Chuang (莊桓山) · Logarchéon Inc.

Abstract

The central question. How can faith and identity be kept intact under pressure that works by controlling moral categories — the labels under which a person is recorded, judged, and treated — and what does the documented record of the Church under communism show about the outcome?

The thesis advanced here. The answer has four clauses. The mechanism is old: coercion that works through administrative categories rests on a structure visible in Genesis 3:22, formalised by Hegel, and extended by Gramsci, and it is limited by natural law and by the lawful judgment of peers. Identity holds where categories do not reach: it rests on continuity, distinctiveness, self-efficacy, and self-esteem (Breakwell 1986), and each can be anchored in sacramental practice and in dated, public work. Documented suppression did not extinguish the faith it targeted: in Poland from 1953 and in Shanghai from 1951 the record shows the opposite. Doctrine sets a prior, not a verdict: Catholic teaching on suicide weighs strongly against an unsupported claim that a practising Catholic is suicidal, but it does not replace evidence about the person.

How the thesis is tested. Each clause is answerable from evidence and can be overturned by evidence. A coercive system shown to control the moral judgment of those it categorises, and not only their records, would refute the first. Evidence that the four principles of identity do not predict resilience under threat would weaken the second. A record from Poland or Shanghai showing a Catholic community contracting under suppression and not recovering would qualify the third. A large cohort in which religious practice showed no association with lower suicide risk would weaken the empirical half of the fourth. Every proposition carries its sources, so that any clause can be checked against the documents that support it.

The mission, set out in Part I. Alongside the analysis stands a practical undertaking: a documentary journal of parish life in Taiwan, written in the third person in the manner of Matteo Ricci's letters, together with a comparative legal study of how an association of the Catholic laity — a Knights of Columbus council — would be founded and governed under canon law, Taiwan's civil law, and the law of its United States origin.

The present position

The Holy See has maintained diplomatic relations with the Republic of China since 1942 and is the only state in Europe to do so. The Sovereign Military Order of Malta, a sovereign subject of international law, maintains diplomatic relations with the Holy See and with more than one hundred states. Since 2018 the Holy See and the People's Republic of China have operated under a Provisional Agreement on the appointment of bishops, renewed in 2020, 2022, and 2024. The Church in Taiwan therefore stands at the point where the Holy See's formal relations and its practical engagement with Beijing meet — and this bears directly on how the mission of Part I should be read.

Summary of findings

The mission of Part I rests on a two-directional obligation and on a legal inventory spanning three jurisdictions: the Internal Revenue Code and Connecticut incorporation on the United States side, the 人民團體法, 財團法人法, and 所得稅法 on the Taiwan side, and canons 298–329 of the Code of Canon Law. Part II shows that the flag in an institutional record is the administrative form of a moral categorisation made without accountability to the moral order, and that five independent literatures on coercive practice — on torture, punitive corrections, transnational repression, metric targets, and learned helplessness — record the same failure. Part III sets out a daily protocol grounded in identity-process theory and in the experimental literature on controllability and stress. Part IV documents Poland and Shanghai: Wyszyński freed in 1956, Solidarity grown to ten million, Bishop Kung's thirty years of imprisonment, and the sentence record of the Shanghai Jesuit network, which shows three patterns — serve and detain, serve and re-arrest, and death in custody. Part V sets out the Catholic doctrine on suicide at seven levels of authority, with the Catechism's teaching on diminished culpability and hope, and replaces a one-question diagnostic with a two-question rule. Claims without public evidence are not asserted, and family testimony is graded as such.

Method and evidence grades

Euclid's order of presentation · Siekiera's decision tree, why-question ladders, and failure modes

What this section is for: the rules of construction in detail — the two methods, the evidence standard, the grades, and the decision tree.

The studies follow two methods. The first is Euclid's: terms are defined before they are used, assumptions are stated as postulates so that they can be examined, and every proposition is followed by a demonstration that names the definitions, postulates, and sources on which it depends. No step is carried by words such as clearly or obviously; where a step rests on inference rather than record, it is graded as inference.

The second is the working method reconstructed from Dr Joanna Siekiera's legal-advisory practice: identify the arena, name the actors, inventory the governing law and doctrine by rank, ask why protection fails, locate the leverage, and translate the analysis into action. Each part opens with a why-question ladder and closes with the failure mode that the analysis is designed to avoid.

Preamble

Definitions, postulates, and common notions

The primitives on which Parts I–V depend

What this section is for: to fix the terms, the assumptions, and the rules of inference before any evidence is argued.

Definitions

Definition 1 · Coercive system

An arrangement of actors, records, and procedures that seeks to change a person's conduct or standing by pressure rather than by lawful judgment: transnational repression, administrative flagging, onset-point behavioural control, and the inversion of the complainant into the suspect.

Definition 2 · Moral category

The classification of an act or a person as good or evil, grounded in the natural law and, through it, in the eternal law (lex aeterna).

Definition 3 · Administrative category

A classification entered in an institutional record by an official or a system, whatever its relation to the moral category it claims to describe.

Definition 4 · Flag

An administrative category applied to a named person and read by every later institution that consults the record.

Definition 5 · 鎮壓 (zhènyā)

State suppression: the apparatus a state deploys to silence or neutralise a person or movement. The same term the Chinese Communist Party uses for the 1989 Tiananmen suppression (六四).

Definition 6 · Onset point

The moment of transition — a departure, a start of a journey, the beginning of a work episode — at which a coercive system applies its aversive provocation.

Definition 7 · Witness

The act of placing an account of events into a dated record that the coercive system did not author.

Definition 8 · Identity components

After Breakwell (2021): continuity, distinctiveness, self-efficacy, and self-esteem.

Definition 9 · Tuitio Fidei · Obsequium Pauperum

The two charisms of the Sovereign Military Order of Malta: the defence of the faith, and service to the poor and the infirm.

Definition 10 · Bridge and Eyes

A two-directional mission: eyes, by which one community sees how another actually works; a bridge, by which that other community becomes accessible rather than merely visible.

Postulates

These are the working assumptions of the studies. Stated openly, each can be examined and contested.

Postulate 1 · Natural law

Moral categories are accessible to human reason through its participation in the eternal law and exist independently of any administrative apparatus (Aquinas, ST I-II, q.91, a.2).

Postulate 2 · Finite resources

Surveillance and suppression consume finite resources, and those who direct them allocate them against perceived threats rather than against negligible actors.

Postulate 3 · Doctrinal binding

For a practising Catholic, the moral teaching of the Magisterium binds conduct.

Postulate 4 · Due process

No person may be deprived of liberty, property, or standing except by the lawful judgment of peers or by the law of the land (Magna Carta, cl. 39; Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Art. 9).

Postulate 5 · Permanence of the dated record

A record that is dated and independently archived cannot be revised retroactively by a third party.

Postulate 6 · Grading

A claim takes the grade of its weakest supporting source.

Common notions

Common Notion 1 · The label is not the thing

A flag is not a fact about the person flagged; it is a fact about the person or system that entered it.

Common Notion 2 · Transmission of warrant

A conclusion receives only as much warrant as its premises and the rule that joins them.

Common Notion 3 · No free therefore

A word such as therefore or obviously does not create an inference; it announces that one should exist.

Common Notion 4 · Contradiction rejects the assumption

If an assumption leads by valid steps to a contradiction, the assumption is rejected.

Common Notion 5 · Completeness by exhaustion

If every alternative but one is excluded, the remaining alternative is established — provided the list of alternatives was complete.

Take-away: the administrative category and the moral category are distinct terms (Definitions 2–3), and every assumption the later parts need is stated here as a postulate.

Bridge and Eyes · Taiwan's Catholic Church and the People of Taiwan

What this part is for: to set out the mission — what is owed in each direction between Taiwan's Church and the universal Church — and the law that any new association of the laity must meet.

Why-question ladder
  1. Why is Taiwan's Church largely invisible to Catholics elsewhere? By geopolitical circumstance rather than fault: its state is formally unacknowledged by most of the world, although the Holy See recognises the Republic of China.
  2. Why does that invisibility run in both directions? Because the workings of the universal Church are equally opaque from Taiwan.
  3. Why the Order of Malta? As a sovereign subject of international law in diplomatic relation with the Holy See and with over one hundred states, it moves through channels that others cannot readily reach.
  4. Why at the parish level? Because that is where the Church's actual condition is most honestly visible.
  5. Why a legal study alongside the pastoral one? Because any new structure of the laity on the island must be lawful in canon law, in Taiwan's civil law, and — if modelled on a United States organisation — in the law of its origin.

Part II · Metaphysical architecture

Genesis 3:22 · Hegel · Gramsci — The Weaponization of Moral Categories

da'at tov va-ra' · List der Vernunft · hegemony · natural law · Magna Carta clause 39

What this part is for: to identify the mechanism by which coercive systems control moral categories, and the counter to it.

Coercive systems — transnational repression (Dukalskis 2021), administrative flagging, onset-point behavioural control, the inversion of the complainant into the suspect — share a root mechanism that is older than any of the academic fields that now study them. It is visible in Genesis 3:22, formalised in Hegel's philosophy of history, extended by Gramsci into a theory of cultural power, and countered — structurally, not merely rhetorically — by the natural law tradition that runs from Aquinas through Magna Carta through the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Understanding the root mechanism is the prerequisite for understanding why coercive practices eventually fail on their own terms (Proposition II.6), and why the resilience framework of Part III constitutes a genuine rather than merely aspirational counter.

Why-question ladder
  1. Why does a flag outweigh the evidence in an institutional encounter? Because the category is in place before the evidence is heard (Proposition II.1).
  2. Why do those who process the flag not question it? Because their energy is consumed by the binary they inhabit (Proposition II.2).
  3. Why does the category feel like a fact? Because it has been installed as common sense (Proposition II.3).
  4. Who benefits? Whoever defines the categories, without appearing as a party to the conflict (Proposition II.4).
  5. What limits that power? Natural law, and the jury of peers that gives it institutional form (Proposition II.5).
Proposition II.1 · Genesis 3:22

Moral categorisation detached from its grounding becomes an instrument, usable by whoever controls the definitions; the flag in an institutional record is the administrative form of that instrument.

Depends on: Definitions 2–4; Postulate 1; Genesis 3:22 [F]

Demonstration · knowledge of good and evil, detached from its grounding

"And the LORD God said, 'The man has now become like one of us, knowing good and evil.'" (Gen 3:22)

וְהָיָה כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ לָדַעַת טוֹב וָרָע

we-hayah ke-akhad mi-menu la-da'at tov va-ra'

The operative phrase is da'at tov va-ra' — the knowledge, or more precisely the capacity to define and apply, good and evil. In the Catholic intellectual tradition this is not mere moral awareness; it is the power to adjudicate moral categories — to determine what counts as good and what counts as evil. The problem in Genesis 3 is not that humans acquired this capacity, but that they acquired it before the corresponding grounding — before the tree of life, before the beatific vision, before the union with divine reason that would give the capacity its proper reference point.

The result is that moral categorisation becomes available as an instrument — detached from its grounding, usable by whoever controls the definitions. The system designer does not need to be overtly violent. It only needs to control the categories: who is labelled good (loyal, legitimate, compliant, protected) and who is labelled evil (dissident, flagged, threatening, mentally unstable). Once those definitions are in place, the naturally occurring human drive to oppose evil — which is genuine, healthy, and not itself the problem — becomes the system's instrument. People fight what they have been told is evil. The designer of the label never appears in the conflict. The categories function automatically. The flag in the institutional record is the administrative form of da'at tov va-ra' wielded without its grounding: it makes the categorisation without being accountable to the moral reality the categorisation claims to describe.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition II.2 · Hegel — List der Vernunft, the ruse of reason

The designer of a moral binary extracts the value of the conflict fought within it, without entering the conflict.

Depends on: Proposition II.1; Hegel, Lectures on the Philosophy of History [F]; 孫子兵法 · 用間 [F]

Demonstration

Hegel's term in the Philosophy of History is List der Vernunft — the ruse, or craft, of reason. Reason (Geist, the dialectical movement of history toward self-knowledge) uses individuals' ambitions and conflicts as its unconscious instrument without those individuals knowing it. Napoleon believed he was pursuing imperial ambition. Hegel, seeing him ride through Jena on 13 October 1806, called him "the world-soul on horseback" (letter to Niethammer). The Philosophy of History reads such figures as instruments of the ruse of reason: Reason using Napoleon, Napoleon serving a synthesis he could not see. The conflict participants' energy is entirely consumed by the binary they inhabit. They have no cognitive resources left to ask who designed the binary.

The thesis–antithesis–synthesis dialectic does not require the thesis and antithesis to know they are serving a synthesis above them. They fight with complete conviction — the conflict is real, the stakes for each participant are genuine. The synthesis that emerges from each conflict cycle belongs to whoever occupies the position of the synthesis. The conflict participants generated the energy; the synthesis-holder extracts the value.

Applied operationally: the coercive system designer creates or exploits a moral binary — patriot versus traitor, legitimate versus flagged, loyal neighbour versus dangerous dissident — and the two sides expend their conflict-energy against each other along lines the designer defined. The five-agent framework of 孫子兵法 · 用間 (因間 · 內間 · 反間 · 死間 · 生間) is the ancient Chinese codification of this: different agent types operate in conflict with each other along axes defined by the campaign designer, while the designer extracts strategic value from the conflict without entering it.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition II.3 · Gramsci — hegemony

A category that has become common sense is invisible as a political intervention; the first counter-hegemonic act is to make the category visible as a category.

Depends on: Propositions II.1–II.2; Gramsci, Prison Notebooks (1929–1935) [F]; Common Notion 1

Demonstration · when the categories become common sense

Antonio Gramsci (a left-Hegelian, writing from Mussolini's prisons, 1929–1935) formalised the mechanism as hegemony: the ruling class maintains power not primarily through overt force but by making its moral framework the senso comune — the common sense — of the entire society, including the governed. The governed think and judge and act within the categories the ruling class provides. They fight genuine conflicts — the moral stakes are real to them — but within a framework that was designed by the ruling class and that always resolves in the ruling class's favour, regardless of which side wins any particular conflict.

The hegemonic system's greatest achievement is making its categories feel natural: the flag feels like a fact about the person, not about the person who authored the flag. The administrative record feels like a description of reality, not a political intervention in reality. The institutional officer who processes the flag believes they are responding to evidence, not inheriting a categorisation. The system's categories have been successfully installed as common sense, and the installation is invisible because common sense does not announce itself as such.

The anti-CCP activist community is a genuine moral community fighting a genuine moral conflict. But if the PRC's United Front apparatus, which directs influence work at overseas Chinese communities (Brady 2017), can successfully inject its categorisation into the institutional common sense of the host state — so that the activist is pre-flagged, the report is inverted, the law enforcement officer processes the complaint as a threat assessment — then the moral energy of the activist community is absorbed by navigating administrative inversions rather than by the original work. Gramsci's insight: the counter-hegemonic movement must first make the categories visible as categories — must demonstrate that what presents itself as common sense is in fact a specific political intervention — before it can contest those categories on their merits.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition II.4 · The synthesis

Whoever defines the moral categories within which conflict occurs occupies, structurally, the position that Genesis 3:22 describes: an architect of the framework rather than a participant in the conflict.

Depends on: Propositions II.1–II.3

Demonstration · who controls the knowledge of good and evil

The connection between Genesis and Hegel is this: whoever controls the knowledge of good and evil in the Hegelian sense — whoever defines the moral categories within which conflict occurs — occupies the position of God in Genesis 3:22. They know good and evil not as participants in the conflict but as architects of the framework within which conflict is adjudicated. They do not fight. They define who fights, on what terrain, under what rules.

The coercive system's designer, structurally:

  • defines who is flagged (evil) and who is protected (good) through administrative categorisation;
  • sets lower echelons fighting within that framework — each convinced they are fighting genuine evil;
  • never appears as a party to the conflict, and contributes no force to it;
  • extracts value (information, resource attrition, population control, institutional capture) from the conflict.

The targeted individual is not fighting a human enemy. The individual is fighting a categorisation — an administrative label that the system placed on the person and that every subsequent institution reads without questioning its source. The real conflict is not between the flagged individual and the institutions; it is between the flagged individual and the designer of the flag. But the flag makes the second conflict invisible by substituting the first. Every institutional encounter absorbs the energy that would otherwise be directed toward the designer. This is the Genesis 3 mechanism, the Hegelian ruse of reason, and the Gramscian hegemony operating simultaneously in the same administrative apparatus.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition II.5 · The counter — natural law, Magna Carta, the jury of peers

The administrative category is not the moral category; no sovereign can monopolise the knowledge of good and evil, and the jury of peers is the institutional refusal of that monopoly.

Depends on: Postulate 1; Postulate 4; Common Notion 1; Magna Carta cl. 39 [F]; the Templar suppression, 1307 [F]

Demonstration

Natural law (Aquinas, derived from Augustine and Aristotle; see also John 1:1 / Λόγος as the source of both natural law and the Logarchéon name): moral categories are not defined by sovereign power. They are accessible to human reason through its participation in the divine reason — lex aeterna, the eternal law — which exists independently of any administrative apparatus. The sovereign cannot make murder good by calling it justice. It cannot make dissent evil by calling it treason. It cannot make a researcher a threat by flagging him in a database. The administrative category is not the moral category; the flag is not the fact; the record is not the reality.

This is the philosophical counter to the Genesis 3:22 weaponization. The knowledge of good and evil is not something the sovereign can monopolise, because it is not the sovereign's to give or withhold — it is grounded in the natural law accessible to any rational person who reasons carefully. The coercive system controls the administrative categories (the flags, the labels, the institutional records). It cannot control the moral categories, which exist independently and by which the system itself can be judged. Gramsci's counter-hegemonic move is here: making visible that the administrative category is a political intervention, not a natural fact, is already the beginning of the counter.

Magna Carta clause 39 is the constitutional instantiation of the natural law counter: nullus liber homo capiatur vel imprisonetur … nisi per legale iudicium parium suorum vel per legem terrae — no free person shall be seized or imprisoned except by the lawful judgment of their peers or by the law of the land. The jury of peers is the institutional mechanism that interposes between the sovereign's moral categorisation and the individual's fate. The sovereign says "this person is evil." Clause 39 says: prove it before a jury of peers, who will apply the moral categories independently, not as the sovereign has defined them. The jury — drawn from the same community as the accused, not appointed by the accuser — is the structural refusal to let da'at tov va-ra' be monopolised by the sovereign.

The Templar suppression (1307) was the paradigm case of this mechanism's failure: Philip IV controlled the trial process, shaped the moral categories (heresy), and had the confessions extracted under torture (Barber 2006) — the KUBARK mechanism seven centuries before KUBARK. The categories invoked were real (heresy was genuinely evil); their application was fraudulent. The moral category was weaponized by controlling the evidence-production process, which is precisely what torture accomplishes — it produces evidence that confirms the pre-existing categorisation rather than evidence that tests it. Philip IV's inquisition and the twenty-first-century administrative flag operate by the same logic: the category is established before the evidence is gathered, and the evidence-gathering process is designed to confirm, not to test.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition II.6 · Why this grounds the efficiency critique of coercive practice

The five efficiency failures of coercive systems are instances of one structural fact: control of the administrative category does not confer control of the moral reality.

Depends on: Propositions II.1–II.5; O'Mara (2015); Andrews & Bonta (1994); Dukalskis (2021); Goodhart (1975); Seligman & Maier (1967) [F]

Demonstration

The five efficiency refutations in the literature (O'Mara on torture, Andrews & Bonta on punitive corrections, Dukalskis on transnational repression backfire, Goodhart's Law on surveillance capitalism, Seligman on learned helplessness) are not independent empirical findings. They are all instances of a single structural problem: the system that claims to control moral categories by controlling administrative categories eventually discovers that the moral categories do not obey the administrative apparatus. The tortured subject does not reliably produce accurate information — because accuracy is a property of the moral-epistemic order, not of the administrative order. The punished offender does not reliably become compliant — because compliance grounded in genuine behavioural change requires engaging the individual as a moral agent, not merely as an administrative subject. The diaspora activist does not reliably become silent — because the moral legitimacy of the advocacy exists independently of the flag the repressing state places on it.

In each case, the system assumes that controlling the administrative category controls the moral reality. Genesis 3:22 — read through Aquinas — says: it does not. The lex aeterna is not the sovereign's to reassign. The efficiency failures are the empirical signature of this theological and philosophical fact.

— Q.E.D.

Failure mode

Contesting the label on its own terms — arguing within the category that the flag supplies — instead of first showing that the category is an administrative entry, authored by someone, and answerable to the moral order it claims to describe.

Take-away: a flag is an administrative entry authored by someone; natural law and the lawful judgment of peers keep the moral category out of the sovereign's monopoly, and the efficiency literature records what happens when that is forgotten.

Part III · Psychological and theological framework

Identity Integrity Under Coercive Systems

Boethius · Thomas More · Bonhoeffer · Frankl · Solzhenitsyn · Breakwell · Seligman · Magna Carta → UDHR Art. 9

What this part is for: to show where identity rests under sustained pressure, and what daily practice keeps it there.

The question this study addresses is not tactical but existential: for those targeted specifically because they did something right — defended human rights, translated suppressed speech, documented institutional corruption, bore witness where witnessing was dangerous — how does a person maintain psychological integrity and live completely without being reshaped by a system designed to reshape? The answer is grounded in the same tradition that Magna Carta represents: an 800-year lineage of individuals and institutions that survived precisely this pressure, intact.

Why-question ladder
  1. Why does a coercive system target identity rather than conduct alone? Because an identity reshaped by the system's category no longer needs to be coerced (Proposition III.2).
  2. Why has the tradition survived such pressure before? Because its witnesses located identity where the system could not reach (Proposition III.1).
  3. Why daily acts rather than resolutions? Because agency is rebuilt by controllable actions (Proposition III.3).
  4. Why watch for danger signs? Because the deficits of learned helplessness can be mistaken for facts about the world (Proposition III.4).
  5. Where does identity finally reside? In the freedom to choose one's stance toward what happens (Proposition III.5).
Proposition III.1 · The tradition, 1215 to 1948

The protection of the person against deprivation without lawful judgment is a single tradition, and those who engage in this work stand in it.

Depends on: Postulate 4; Magna Carta cl. 39; UDHR Art. 9; British Library (2015) [F]

Demonstration

Magna Carta's clause 39 — nullus liber homo: no free person shall be seized, imprisoned, dispossessed, outlawed, or exiled except by the lawful judgment of peers or by the law of the land — was later read as the foundation of habeas corpus and trial by jury, and its principle passed into Article 9 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). Gandhi invoked it in his farewell letter of 1914, and Nelson Mandela in his Rivonia Trial statement of 1964 (British Library 2015). The suppression of the Knights Templar by Philip IV in 1307 was the paradigm case of what clause 39 later prohibited: imprisonment, dispossession, and exile by sovereign fiat without lawful judgment of peers. Engaging in this work means standing in that lineage. The framework is not being invented — it has already survived monarchs, inquisitions, gulags, and concentration camps.

Four witnesses from that tradition whose situations are directly instructive:

Boethius523–524 AD

Wrote The Consolation of Philosophy in prison awaiting execution by Theodoric. His argument: Fortune can remove wealth, office, reputation, and freedom of movement — but cannot reach the vera bona, the true goods: virtue, understanding, and orientation of the will toward the Good. The coercive system controls the outer perimeter. It cannot reach the interior one unless the person yields it.

Thomas More1535

Deprived of every office and title, imprisoned in the Tower, tried on the testimony of a single hostile witness, executed for refusing to bend his conscience to Henry VIII's redefinition of sovereignty. His identity remained complete at the moment of his death. The system took everything except the one thing it could not reach.

Dietrich BonhoefferTegel Prison, 1944

Wrote the poem Wer bin ich? ("Who Am I?") while held awaiting trial. The poem lists what others see — a calm, cheerful man who speaks to his guards freely and plainly, as though it were his place to command — then what he felt within — restless, ill, trembling, empty — then resolves both: the questioner, whoever he is, belongs to God, who knows him. Identity is not the external presentation the system reads. It is not even the internal turmoil the system produces. It is the relationship to what cannot be taken.

Viktor FranklTheresienstadt, Auschwitz, Dachau subcamps, 1942–45

Observed that fellow prisoners oriented toward a meaning to be fulfilled were more likely to endure than those who had lost their reason to go on. The primary human drive, in his account, is neither the drive for enjoyment (Freud) nor the drive for power (Adler) but the will to meaning — and meaning, unlike enjoyment or power, cannot be confiscated by an external force (Frankl 1959).

Solzhenitsyn maintained identity through the act of bearing witness: writing what he saw, in secret, over years, knowing he might not survive to publish it. The writing was not merely political — it was the act by which he refused to let the system's version of events become the only version.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition III.2 · The identity architecture — four principles, four lines of attack

A coercive system can press on all four principles of identity at once; naming the principle under pressure makes the pressure legible rather than overwhelming.

Depends on: Definition 8; Breakwell (1986) [F]

Demonstration

Identity Process Theory (Breakwell 1986) identifies four principles that govern the structure of identity: continuity, distinctiveness, self-efficacy, and self-esteem. A threat to identity is a threat to one or more of them. Understanding which principle is under pressure at any given moment makes the pressure legible rather than overwhelming.

PrincipleLine of attackCounter
1 · ContinuityAbrupt, discontinuous events designed to make the period before targeting feel like a different life from the period after.The liturgical calendar, daily Mass, and ongoing work all assert continuity — the same person who attended Mass this morning attended last Sunday and will attend next Sunday. No external system can redact biographical memory.
2 · DistinctivenessThe system's category — target, threat, flagged individual — is offered as the only available self-description.An identity built from vocation, faith, scholarship, and family lineage is irreducible to any administrative category, and predates every such category by decades.
3 · Self-efficacyPressure applied at onset points — making each departure feel dangerous — erodes the belief that action produces outcomes.Every completed piece of work, every report filed, every dated record is an efficacy event. Work that is public and timestamped cannot be retroactively confiscated.
4 · Self-esteemThe inversion mechanism — making the complainant a suspect, the victim an aggressor in the account of their own victimisation.The Catholic framework of human dignity and the Magna Carta tradition both assert that the value of a human person is not adjudicated by the state, is not subject to administrative flags, and does not depend on any institution's assessment.

Proposition III.3 · Daily protocol — practical immunisation acts

Controllable daily actions rebuild the sense of agency that uncontrollable pressure erodes; they are defensive infrastructure, not aspiration.

Depends on: Proposition III.2; Postulate 5; Seligman & Maier (1967); McEwen (2007) [F]

Demonstration

Experience of control protects against later helplessness: animals first given controllable stress did not develop helplessness when later subjected to uncontrollable stress (Seligman & Maier 1967). The acts below are the human equivalent — controllable actions that rebuild the sense of agency that uncontrollable pressure is designed to erode.

Sacramental anchor

Daily Mass is, in the identity resilience framework, a continuity anchor: a daily act that places the self within a narrative vastly larger than the coercive system's frame, performed in community, in a known building, at a known time, regardless of what happened on the way there.

Witness document

Solzhenitsyn wrote. Bonhoeffer wrote. Frankl reconstructed his manuscript in the camps. Dated, independently archived work — preprints with persistent identifiers, published scholarship — places a person's account of events into a record that the system did not author. Each archived work is a dated stake in the ground: this person was here, was doing this work, at this time. By Postulate 5, no third party can revise it retroactively.

Fixed routine

Coercive pressure exploits the transition moment — the onset point. The counter is to give each transition a fixed internal structure — the same preparation, the same documentation protocol prepared in advance — so that a provocation loses its novelty effect. The routine governs the sequence of preparation, not the pattern of movement: the route and timing of travel follow ordinary protective-security practice, which varies them.

Physical baseline

Frankl recorded that prisoners who kept humour and attention to small beauties bore the camps better (Frankl 1959). This has a physiological basis: chronic stress raises glucocorticoid levels, suppresses neurogenesis in the hippocampus, and can impair memory (McEwen 2007). Sleep, food, exercise, and small sensory comforts are not luxuries under sustained pressure. They are defensive infrastructure maintaining the biological substrate of identity itself.

Community

The most dangerous isolation is not physical but epistemic: being the only person who holds one's account of events. Sacramental community — the parish, the confessional, the Order — provides a non-institutional, non-state-mediated witness relationship. The priest in confession is not a law enforcement officer. The parish is not a flagging system. This is structurally different from any institutional encounter mediated by a record.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition III.4 · Danger signs — when the pressure is succeeding

The three deficits of learned helplessness, once recognised as effects of what is being done rather than as facts about the world, lose their hold.

Depends on: Abramson, Seligman & Teasdale (1978); Proposition III.3 [F]

Demonstration

Learned helplessness presents as three deficits — motivational, cognitive, and emotional (Abramson, Seligman & Teasdale 1978). Recognising them as symptoms of what is being done rather than as facts about the world is the critical cognitive move.

DeficitPresentationResponse
MotivationalReluctance to begin journeys, activities, or work episodes — feeling that starting is dangerous.Not bravado, but noting the reluctance itself, and then beginning regardless, with the prepared protocol.
CognitiveDifficulty recognising genuine escape routes or available resources — assuming everything is hostile, failing to distinguish a real threat from ordinary events, not using legal counsel or other help that is genuinely available.Deliberately listing the controllable options and the resources at hand, and testing each perceived threat against evidence before acting on it.
Emotional — the most serious markerA flattened response to things that normally bring fulfilment: progress in work, liturgical beauty, discovery. When the work no longer interests and Mass no longer grounds, the attrition itself is the emergency, not any specific incident within it.Seek sacramental counsel and a clinician — preferably a therapist familiar with the wellbeing of human rights defenders.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition III.5 · The deepest counter — the last of the human freedoms

Identity resides in the freedom to choose one's stance toward what happens; a coercive system can shape events but cannot occupy that choice.

Depends on: Propositions III.1–III.4; Frankl (1959); Bernard of Clairvaux, De Laude Novae Militiae [F]

Demonstration

Frankl named it the last of the human freedoms: to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances, to choose one's own way (Frankl 1959). Between what happens to a person and how that person responds lies the space in which that choice is made. Pressure can occupy the event. It cannot occupy the space, and it cannot occupy the response. Both remain available, as long as the person knows what they are and refuses to yield them unreflectively.

The tradition from Boethius through Magna Carta through Thomas More through Bonhoeffer through Frankl through Mandela is a single tradition about that space. Bernard of Clairvaux's De Laude Novae Militiae (written c. 1128–1136) describes the knight who is free from fear of death because death cannot reach what he has already given to God. That is not a metaphor for courage. It is a structural claim about where identity resides: within that space, not in the external perimeter the system controls.

Documenting events precisely is itself an exercise of that freedom. The record then exists, and the system did not author it.

— Q.E.D.

Failure mode

Accepting the system's category as a self-description, or answering attrition with bravado instead of documentation; both surrender the space in which identity resides.

Take-away: identity rests on four principles that no administrative category reaches; controllable daily acts, community, and dated work keep them in place, and the deficits of helplessness are signs to act on, not facts about the world.

Proposition IV.1 · 鎮壓 as confirmation of effectiveness — the intelligence inference [I — consistent with documented doctrine]

The intensity of the suppression apparatus deployed against a target encodes the system's own assessment of that target's impact or potential impact.

Depends on: Definition 5; Postulate 2

Demonstration

Surveillance and harassment operations are resource-intensive — personnel, informants, record-keeping, coordination — and resources are finite. They are allocated against perceived threats, not against negligible actors. The intensity of the 鎮壓 (state suppression — the same term the CCP uses for the 1989 Tiananmen suppression, 六四) apparatus deployed against a target is therefore itself an intelligence indicator: it encodes the system's assessment of the target's impact or potential impact. A researcher who generates no threat assessment generates no 鎮壓. A person against whom such an apparatus has been activated is a person the system has assessed as capable of producing outcomes it wants to prevent.

The Soviet record shows the principle from the other side. KGB files in the Mitrokhin archive document grave concern with the pontificate of John Paul II: a 1980 KGB report described his election as an attempt to "divide socialism from the inside," and the KGB answered with disinformation campaigns and attempts to penetrate the pope's circle (Brennan 2022). The countermeasures are the KGB's own record of the threat it perceived [I — by Postulate 2]. Ronald Reagan, watching John Paul II's 1979 visit to Poland before his presidency, is reported to have drawn the parallel inference: "I have had a feeling, particularly in the pope's visit to Poland, that religion may turn out to be the Soviets' Achilles' heel" (Shea 2023, from the oral history of William Clark) [D].

Applied: where a 鎮壓 apparatus is documented against a person or a line of work, its existence is not evidence of weakness. It is evidence that the system has assessed that work as posing a genuine threat to its interests. The correct interpretation of documented 鎮壓 is not discouragement. It is confirmation that the work has reached the threshold at which the system must respond. The correct response is not to stop. It is to document more carefully, to publish more systematically, and to understand the structure of the operation well enough to make it visible .

Proposition IV.2 · More 鎮壓, more faith — Tertullian's formula [F — 2nd century; confirmed by historical record]

Suppression, intended as a deterrent, acts as a multiplier for faith, because faith propagates by witness rather than by platform.

Depends on: Definition 7; Tertullian, Apologeticum 50.13 [F]

Demonstration

Tertullian of Carthage, writing c. 197 AD when Roman persecution of Christians was systematic, stated the principle in the closing chapter of the Apologeticum (50.13): the blood of Christians becomes the Church's new growth. The formula identifies a structural property of faith under suppression: the martyrs' witness, which the Roman system intended as a deterrent, functioned as an exponential multiplier for the Church. Suppression creates visibility; visibility creates witness; witness creates community; community creates institutions that outlast the suppressor. The Roman Empire no longer exists. The Church it tried to suppress does.

The mechanism is structural, not merely moral: the suppressor's use of 鎮壓 assumes that the target's influence is a function of personal freedom of movement and access to institutional platforms. Remove those, and the influence diminishes. This assumption is wrong. Faith — and the intellectual work it grounds — does not require institutional platforms to propagate. It requires witness. And the act of suppression is itself a form of involuntary witness: it makes the suppressor visible, demonstrates the threat it perceives, and elevates the target's standing in the eyes of every observer who draws the correct inference from the existence of the repression. A documented act of suppression, recorded with date and place, is Tertullian's formula in modern form: the 鎮壓 becomes new growth.

This is the theological answer to the DDD framework (Farber, Harlow & West 1957) taken up in the KUBARK manual (Central Intelligence Agency 1963). Debility, Dependency, Dread operate on the assumption that the target's will to continue is a function of its psychological resources, and that those resources can be exhausted. Faith adds a source of will that is not exhaustible by external attrition — not because it is superstitious but because it is anchored in a relationship with the divine that is constitutively outside the 鎮壓 system's reach. Boethius's vera bona, Frankl's last of the human freedoms, and the Tertullian formula are three names for the same structural fact.

— Q.E.D.

Proposition IV.3 · Poland — the most documented successful case [F]

In Poland the suppression of the Church strengthened the faith it targeted and prepared the institutional template of Solidarity.

Depends on: Propositions IV.1–IV.2

Demonstration

Cardinal Stefan Wyszyński — imprisoned by the communist regime in 1953, freed in 1956 — emerged from prison institutionally stronger than before. The communists could not quench the faith of the Polish people; on the contrary, the nation's heroic primate made that faith stronger. His framework of resistance through the Church's institutional continuity — maintaining sacramental life, the liturgical calendar, and Catholic education under systematic state pressure — established the template that Solidarity would later operationalise.

When John Paul II kissed the ground at the Warsaw airport on 2 June 1979, the historian John Lewis Gaddis judges that he began the process that would end communism in Poland and, ultimately, elsewhere in Europe (Gaddis 2005). The mechanism was not political but anthropological. In George Weigel's formulation [D], "Poland was not a communist country; Poland was a Catholic nation saddled with a communist state" — and seven hours of papal presence in Warsaw made that truth visible to millions simultaneously. Within months, Solidarity's enrolment grew from zero to ten million.

Blessed Jerzy Popiełuszko — an anti-communist priest who supported the Solidarność movement, murdered for his outspoken advocacy after being kidnapped and tortured by the secret services — became a more powerful symbol after his martyrdom than he was in his ministry. The communist secret police's 鎮壓 of Popiełuszko did not silence the resistance; it accelerated it. Tertullian's formula, confirmed in 1984.

Underground Solidarity received assistance from the United States, including funds channelled through the AFL-CIO and the National Endowment for Democracy; Vatican support has also been reported (Bernstein 1992) [D].

Proposition IV.4 · Cardinal Ignatius Kung Pin-mei 龔品梅 · Bishop of Shanghai — the Chinese Catholic parallel [F]

Thirty years of CCP suppression of Bishop Kung did not destroy the underground church; it defined it.

Depends on: Propositions IV.1–IV.2

Demonstration

The given name carries its own emblem: 梅 (mei) is the winter-flowering blossom, which opens under conditions that kill other plants. Ignatius Kung Pin-mei (龔品梅, 1901–2000) served as Bishop of Shanghai from 1950 until his death. He spent 30 years in prison for defying attempts by the Chinese Communist Party to control Catholics through the government-approved Chinese Catholic Patriotic Association. The CCP's end goal was to isolate Chinese Catholics from Rome and create an independent "patriotic" Church — a multi-pillar strategy of expropriation of Church property, thought-reform curriculum, a surveillance apparatus monitoring the clergy, and an intense propaganda campaign to discredit foreign mission clergy and delegitimise the Holy Father. The same structure recurs in the cases recorded below.

On 8 September 1955, Bishop Kung was arrested along with more than 200 priests and Catholic leaders of the laity in Shanghai. The CCP announced he would publicly confess his crimes. He appeared in Shanghai's stadium, his hands restrained, pushed toward a microphone. When he reached it, he shouted: "Long live Christ the King! Long live the Pope!" The crowd — thousands of Catholics compelled to attend — responded: "Long live Christ the King! Long live Bishop Kung!" The event was suspended and Kung taken back to jail.

During his 30 years of imprisonment, Kung recited the Mass in Latin from memory and prayed the rosary without beads, keeping count by hand, forbidden to say Mass or read the Bible or correspond with anyone. Pope John Paul II secretly created him a Cardinal in 1979 — while he was still in prison. When Cardinal Kung finally received his red biretta publicly in June 1991, the 90-year-old raised himself from his wheelchair, set aside his cane, and walked up the steps to kneel at the feet of the Holy Father. The Pope lifted him up, conferred the cardinal's hat upon him, and stood as 9,000 guests in the Vatican audience hall gave a seven-minute standing ovation.

Before his arrest, Bishop Kung had personally supervised volunteers of the Legion of Mary to train catechists to pass on the faith when all the priests were gone. He declared 1952 a Marian Year in Shanghai, with uninterrupted round-the-clock recitation of the rosary before a statue of Our Lady of Fatima that toured all Shanghai's parishes. The statue arrived at Christ the King Church — a church where a major arrest of priests had occurred one month before. Bishop Kung led the rosary there while hundreds of armed police looked on. The 鎮壓 apparatus was present and visible. He prayed the rosary regardless.

Cardinal Kung emerged from prison as a symbol of the underground church's loyalty to the Vatican and resistance against communism. The CCP's 30-year suppression of him did not destroy the underground church. It defined it. The Church he served has survived 70+ years of CCP repression and continues to operate. He is buried at the Santa Clara Mission in California, as is Archbishop Dominic Tang Yee-ming of Guangzhou (Wikipedia, "Dominic Tang") — another Chinese bishop who refused the CCP's categories and paid the same price. The tradition they held is the same tradition that the author's 中華 thesis traces.

One predecessor deserves explicit naming: Fr. Beda Chang, SJ (張伯達, 1905–1951) — the Chinese Jesuit considered the first Catholic martyr of Shanghai under the CCP, who died in prison in November 1951 after torture. The clandestine catechism programme that became the structural backbone of the underground church — the network that allowed the faith to be transmitted when all the priests were gone — was an initiative first spearheaded by Beda Chang. Kung took that initiative, systematised it, and scaled it across the entire Shanghai diocese. The underground church that survived Kung's 30-year imprisonment was built on foundations Beda Chang set in place before his own arrest. Chang pioneered the method; Kung institutionalised it. The Church Kung emerged from prison to lead in 1985 was, in its deep structure, the Church Beda Chang had prepared to survive without its clergy.

Part V · Doctrinal study

Catholic Doctrine on Suicide and the Misattribution of Suicidality

Seven authority layers · the complete record of the Catechism · three wrongs of false attribution · a diagnostic rule for analysts

What this part is for: to state the Catholic doctrine on suicide completely, and to give analysts a sound test for claims that a practising Catholic is suicidal.

Catholic doctrine prohibits suicide. The prohibition is not a personal preference or a cultural convention; it is a moral teaching with the weight of Scripture, an ecumenical council, the Catechism, papal teaching, canon law, and the Scholastic tradition supporting it. The same teaching also recognises the limits of culpability and does not despair of the salvation of those who have taken their own lives. Both halves of the record are set out below.

Why-question ladder
  1. Why does the doctrine matter to a threat assessment? Because a practising Catholic is bound by it (Postulate 3), which bears on how likely a given claim about that person is.
  2. How firmly is it held? At every level of authority, from Scripture to the Doctors (Proposition V.1).
  3. What wrong does a false attribution commit? Three at once (Proposition V.2).
  4. How can an analyst test such a claim? By asking what the claimant knows of the doctrine, and what evidence the claimant holds about the person (Proposition V.3).
Proposition V.1 · The doctrinal prohibition — seven authority layers [F]

The prohibition of suicide is held at every level of Catholic authority, set out here in order of rank, together with the Church's teaching on culpability and hope.

Depends on: Postulate 3

Demonstration

LayerAuthority
1 · ScriptureThe Fifth Commandment prohibits killing (Ex 20:13; Dt 5:17). The Catholic tradition reads it as including the killing of oneself.
2 · Vatican IIGaudium et Spes 27 condemns "whatever is opposed to life itself, such as any type of murder, genocide, abortion, euthanasia, or wilful self-destruction" — the Council names self-destruction explicitly alongside murder, calling all such acts "infamies."
3 · Papal teachingEvangelium Vitae (John Paul II, 1995), §66: "Suicide is always as morally objectionable as murder." §20: to claim the right to abortion, infanticide, and euthanasia, and to recognise that right in law, "means to attribute to human freedom a perverse and evil significance: that of an absolute power over others and against others."
4 · Catechism"Suicide contradicts the natural inclination of the human being to preserve and perpetuate his life. It is gravely contrary to the just love of self. It likewise offends love of neighbour because it unjustly breaks the ties of solidarity with family, nation, and other human societies to which we continue to have obligations. Suicide is contrary to love for the living God" (CCC 2281). "We are stewards, not owners, of the life God has entrusted to us. It is not ours to dispose of" (CCC 2280). The Catechism adds that grave psychological disturbances, anguish, or grave fear of hardship, suffering, or torture can diminish the responsibility of the one who commits suicide (CCC 2282), and that the Church does not despair of the eternal salvation of those who have taken their own lives and prays for them (CCC 2283).
5 · Canon lawThe 1917 Code denied ecclesiastical burial to those who had deliberately taken their own lives (c. 1240 §1, 3°). The 1983 Code omits that provision; it denies funeral rites only to manifest sinners whose funeral would cause public scandal (c. 1184 §1, 3°).
6 · AquinasSuicide is contrary to natural inclination (self-preservation), to charity toward oneself, to the community (which loses one of its own unjustly), and to God's lordship — "it belongs to God alone to pronounce sentence of death and life" (ST II-II, q.64, a.5). Aquinas also answers the objection that killing the righteous does them less harm because they pass at once to glory: that benefit is accidental to the act, and the one who kills still sins (ST II-II, q.64, a.6, ad 2).
7 · FathersAugustine (De Civitate Dei I.17–27) devotes eleven chapters to refuting the idea that suicide can be virtuous — directly against the Roman Stoic tradition and against the claim that Christian women who survived violence should have killed themselves. He concludes that no one may take their own life to escape temporal troubles. This is the oldest sustained Catholic argument against suicide in the Western tradition.

A Catholic may joke about death. Gallows humour is a human universal, and Catholics are not excluded from it. For an informed and practising Catholic, the prohibition on self-killing is as constitutive of the faith as the prohibition on idolatry: doctrine commits the believer to preserving life as a gift from God, to accepting the obligation of continued existence for the sake of family, community, and the common good, and to regarding self-killing as a grave sin against charity, justice, and divine lordship. The empirical record is consistent with this: in a cohort of 89,708 women in the United States followed from 1996 to 2010, those who attended religious services at least weekly had about one-fifth the risk of suicide of women who never attended (VanderWeele et al. 2016). Lower risk is not zero risk; CCC 2282 acknowledges as much.

Proposition V.2 · Three wrongs of false attribution

To characterise a practising Catholic as suicidal, or as a potential suicide bomber, without evidence about that person is to commit three distinct wrongs simultaneously.

Depends on: Proposition V.1; CCC 2284–2285, 2476–2477 [F]

Demonstration

  1. Rejection of the person's belief. The characterisation assumes that the Catholic's doctrinal commitments are either insincere or irrelevant — an affront to the person's dignity as a rational moral agent who has freely committed to a tradition that prohibits the attributed act.
  2. Denial of the person's faith. Made without evidence, the characterisation treats the person's faith as so weak or so hollow that it does not govern the person's actions. This is a form of religious contempt.
  3. Scandal against the Catholic faith itself. The characterisation associates Catholicism with suicide and terrorism in the public mind, producing the "attitude or behaviour which leads another to do evil" that CCC 2284 identifies as scandal. The scandal takes on "particular gravity by reason of the authority of those who cause it" (CCC 2285) — when officials, analysts, or media figures make the characterisation, the authority of their position amplifies the damage. A knowingly false characterisation is, in addition, false witness and calumny (CCC 2476–2477).

— Q.E.D.

Proposition V.3 · Diagnostic rule for intelligence analysis

A claim that a practising Catholic is suicidal requires evidence about that person; doctrine lowers the prior probability of the claim, and the claimant's knowledge of doctrine bears on how the claim should be read.

Depends on: Propositions V.1–V.2; Common Notion 2; Common Notion 5

Demonstration

By Common Notion 5, a conclusion reached by exhaustion holds only if the list of alternatives is complete. A test that asks only whether the claimant knows Catholic doctrine leaves one alternative unexamined: the claimant may know the doctrine and hold evidence about the particular person. The rule therefore has two questions.

QuestionAnswerConsequence
1 · Does the claimant know Catholic doctrine on suicide?NoThe claim may reflect ignorance. Present CCC 2280–2283, ST II-II q.64 a.5, Augustine De Civitate Dei I.17–27, and Gaudium et Spes 27. If the claimant accepts the correction, the matter is resolved as an error.
2 · What evidence about this person supports the claim?NoneA claimant who knows the doctrine and holds no person-specific evidence is making an unsupported attribution against the prior that the doctrine establishes. The basis and motive of the claim should be examined.
Person-specific evidenceThe evidence is assessed on its merits, as for any person; the doctrine does not settle the case, and CCC 2282 itself recognises that grave disturbance can occur.
If a knowingly false claim is used to justify harmScandal (CCC 2284–2287), false witness and calumny (CCC 2476–2477; Ex 20:16), and, where life is endangered, grave injustice against life (CCC 2258–2262).

See also: Part XIV, Propositions MP-12 & MP-13

— Q.E.D.

Failure mode

Accepting a suicidality profile of a practising Catholic at face value — or rejecting person-specific evidence on doctrinal grounds alone. Doctrine sets the prior; evidence decides the case.

Take-away: doctrine weighs heavily against an unsupported attribution of suicidality, and the Church's teaching on diminished culpability and hope belongs to the same record; evidence about the person decides the case.
