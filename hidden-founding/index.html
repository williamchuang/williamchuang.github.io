The Hidden Founding: Catholic Agency and Irony in the Making of the United States, 1048–2025

A revolution that denounced Catholic toleration was won with Catholic money, ships and soldiers. The first American bishop owed his appointment to a Protestant deist he had nursed through illness. A religious order that Rome had abolished survived in the open in Maryland because the new republic disclaimed all authority over religion. This page documents that record in one strictly chronological sequence, with every contested claim travelling alongside its refutation rather than being silently dropped.

Abstract

This page documents the Catholic contribution to the founding of the United States — decisive, largely absent from the national narrative, and repeatedly ironic. A revolution that denounced Catholic toleration was won with Catholic money, ships and soldiers. The first American bishop owed his appointment to a Protestant deist he had nursed through illness. A religious order that Rome had abolished survived in the open in Maryland, and under Russian protection, because the new republic disclaimed all authority over religion. The analytic spine is a licensing model — piracy, privateering and chartered monopoly as three states of one armed commercial system, and papal bulls, royal charters and letters of marque as instruments of the same kind — which explains the whole sequence without appeal to hidden agency. A graded appendix separates what the record supports from what it refutes, including Fawcett's East India Company flag thesis, the Essene origin of the skull and crossbones, the Carroll land donation, and the three-cities sovereignty claim.

The Priest and the Deist

In the late spring of 1776 a man of seventy lay ill on the road south from Montreal. The mission had failed completely: the Canadians would not join the American union, and the reason was on paper in two documents Congress had issued weeks apart — one denouncing the Quebec Act for tolerating Catholicism in the harshest terms available, the other inviting Catholic Canadians to make common cause. They had read both.

Benjamin Franklin, the most celebrated American alive, was nursed back to Philadelphia by a Catholic priest. John Carroll belonged to an order Rome had abolished three years earlier, and came from a colony where saying Mass in public was a crime and Catholics could not vote, hold office, practise law, or run a school.

Eight years later, in Paris, Rome asked the American government how the Catholics of the new republic should be governed. Congress answered in May 1784 that it held no authority whatever over religion and could neither approve nor object. The reply was meant as constitutional principle. Its unintended effect was to leave Franklin's private opinion as the only American input Rome possessed — and he recommended the priest who had nursed him. On 6 June 1784 John Carroll was named Superior of the Mission. In 1789 he became the first Catholic bishop in the United States, with jurisdiction from the Atlantic to the Mississippi, and founded Georgetown.

The shape of the whole story
The first head of the Catholic Church in America was, in practice, chosen by a Protestant deist, because a legislature had declined to have a religious policy.

This page is an attempt to document that pattern at length. Four ironies recur.

IRONY I

The republic was rescued by the powers it had denounced

France supplied the navy, the subsidies and the loans; Spain took the Gulf; Havana raised the money for Yorktown in a matter of hours; de Grasse, trained in the galleys of the Order of Malta, closed the Chesapeake. A revolution whose founding grievances included Catholic toleration was decided by Catholic monarchies.

IRONY II

Each shaped the other's institutions

Franklin's recommendation governed the American church for a generation; Charles Carroll, barred from voting in his own colony, signed the Declaration with his address attached; Daniel Carroll and Thomas FitzSimons signed the Constitution.

IRONY III

A suppressed order survived here in the open

From 1773 to 1814 the Society of Jesus did not canonically exist. In Maryland its former members held roughly 12,000 acres under a state charter, ran a college, and from 1805 lived under the Russian province — because an Orthodox empress had refused to publish a papal brief, and because a republic that could not establish a religion could not abolish one either.

IRONY IV

The machinery of revolution was built to police piracy

Vice-admiralty jurisdiction without juries entered colonial life in 1698 as a remedy in the East India Company's interest. Seventy years later it was a grievance in the Declaration.

None of this requires a hidden hand. Every link is a public instrument — a bull, a charter, a statute, a treaty, a letter — and the documentary record is stranger than the conspiratorial one. The concealment is not in the events but in the telling: these facts were not suppressed, they were simply left out of the national story, and the point of the timeline that follows is to put them back in one sequence where they can be checked.

01

Core Finding: Licence, Not Conspiracy

In brief

Four institutions that look unrelated — the knightly orders, the Jesuits, the East India Company, and the American founding — are joined by a single mechanism. Each depended on a licence granted by a sovereign: a papal bull, a royal charter, a letter of marque. When the licensing power turned against them, the ones that survived did so by moving the licence somewhere else. The ones that could not move it disappeared.

M1 · TEMPLAR ASSETS

Relocated into a crown order

Suppressed in 1312, the Temple's Iberian property and personnel passed in 1317 and 1319 to Montesa and the Order of Christ — royal creations under crown administration from 1523. Prince Henry's voyages were funded by those revenues. No surviving fleet, no hidden succession.

Grade A
M2 · THE SOCIETY OF JESUS

Relocated into an Orthodox empire

Suppressed worldwide in 1773 by a brief extracted by Catholic crowns, the Society continued at Polotsk because Catherine II declined to promulgate it, with superiors and a novitiate, for forty-one years. Restoration in 1814 extended a living body rather than reviving a dead one.

Grade A
M3 · THE ORDER OF ST JOHN

Surrendered territory, retained sovereignty

Stripped of its French commanderies in 1792 and of Malta in 1798, rescued uncanonically by Paul I and left stateless in 1801, the Order settled in Rome in 1834 and had its Grand Mastership restored in 1879. It holds extraterritorial premises — Palazzo Malta and the Villa del Priorato on the Aventine, and Fort St Angelo since 1998 — but no sovereign territory, and it never recovered the island.

Grade A
M4 · THE EAST INDIA COMPANY

Never suppressed, and left no successor

Statutorily stripped of its monopolies in 1813 and 1833, it ceased trading in 1834 and was dissolved in 1874. What passed to Jardine Matheson, Dent, Russell & Co and the Bombay houses was the deregulated trade and its function, not the corporation.

Grade A
The defensible model
Piracy, privateering and chartered monopoly were three licensing states of one armed commercial system, and the boundary between them was drawn by whoever held the pen. Institutions outlive the states that suppress them by moving the licence; institutions with no principle beyond extraction do not outlive the charter.

Strand tags. Method. Entries are ordered strictly by date, to the day where an instrument supplies one and to the hour in the three places where hours are known and bear on the reading. Era headings serve navigation only. Where a span is all the record gives, the entry appears at the start of that span. Nothing has been ordered for narrative effect, and the causal arguments in Part II are built from entries a reader can check against Part I rather than from proximity alone.

Terms. "Suppression" means the canonical act of 1773 (Dominus ac Redemptor), not expulsion by a state, which is what Portugal did in 1759, France in 1764, and Spain in 1767. "Restoration," for the Order of St John, has two senses that must be kept apart: territorial restoration, provided for at Amiens in 1802 and never carried out, and institutional restoration, accomplished at Rome in 1834 and 1879. "Country traders" were private merchants licensed by the Company to carry cargoes it would not carry itself. "Privateer" denotes a commerce raider holding a commission; "pirate" denotes one who does not, and much of Part II concerns who held the power to make that distinction.

On the record's hard parts. This page reports documented violence and documented injury with their causes, including the Bengal famine, the atrocities aboard the Ganj-i-Sawai, the Gordon Riots, the enslaved labor of the Maryland Jesuit estates and the 1838 sale, and the disenfranchisement of the District's residents. Omitting them would misstate how these institutions actually worked.
03

The Licence Thesis

In brief

The East India Company's charter granted it the right to make war, sign treaties, coin money and take prizes. A pirate did the same things without a charter. The difference was legal, not behavioral — which is why the same act could be a crime, a public service, or an act of state depending on who was describing it.

The chartered raider. The Company chartered on 31 December 1600 was not a purely commercial body. James Lancaster, who commanded the first voyage, had been a privateer against Spain. Between 1686 and 1690, under Sir Josiah Child, the Company made open war on the Mughal Empire, seizing Mughal shipping and preying on Red Sea pilgrim traffic — corporately, under charter, what Henry Every's men would be hanged for doing privately five years later.

The boundary made legal. Every's capture of the Ganj-i-Sawai on 7 September 1695 brought Aurangzeb to close the Company's factories at Surat, Bombay, Broach, Agra and Ahmedabad and imprison its officers. The Company's losses were real and its disavowal sincere; what the episode shows is that the most informed commercial court in Asia treated English pirates and the English company as one body. The Company's answer was to make the distinction explicit and to police it: convoy escorts, a doubled bounty, and the piracy acts of 1698 and 1700 allowing vice-admiralty trials in the colonies without juries.

The boundary aimed. Once it existed it could be used. Before 1698 the Company prosecuted lawful English interlopers as pirates. Kanhoji Angre held a commission from his own state and was called a pirate for taxing Company shipping. The Qawasim of the Gulf were given a coast named for the accusation and campaigned against in 1809 and 1819, on a charge that scholarship — including al-Qasimi's — argues was manufactured to remove a commercial rival.

Absorption rather than extinction. Morgan became Lieutenant Governor of Jamaica; Woodes Rogers, a privateer, became Governor of the Bahamas and suppressed Nassau in 1718; the amnesties of 1698 and 1717 returned pirate crews to naval and Company service. Piracy did not end in the 1720s so much as it was licensed, and the licensing coincided with the consolidation of chartered trade.

UnlicensedPiracy. Hanged when caught; pardoned in general amnesties; fenced through colonial ports.
CommissionedPrivateering. The same acts under a letter of marque; the American republic's first instrument at sea.
CharteredMonopoly. War, treaty, coinage and prize-taking granted corporately, with an army and a revenue.
What this thesis does not claim. It does not claim that pirates worked for the Company, which fails on dates and is refuted by the Every episode directly. It claims that the categories were assigned by charter-holders and that the assignment served commercial ends. See §18 for the sorting of the stronger and weaker forms of the argument.

Bengal to Baltimore: The Fiscal Chain

In brief

The first Catholic bishop of the United States owed his appointment, through five documented links, to a land-revenue settlement made in Bengal in 1765 by men who had never heard of him. Every link is a public instrument. No hidden agency is required, and that is the point.

On causation. Every link is a documented, public, institutional act: a revenue grant, a bankruptcy, a statute, a treaty, a letter. The chain is astonishing in its span and unremarkable in its mechanics. When a story of this shape is available, the burden on any hidden-agency account rises sharply — it must explain what the ordinary account leaves unexplained, and here very little remains.

On religious liberty. The American separation of church and state was not, in 1784, primarily an idea that triumphed. It was a legislature's confession of incapacity, and that incapacity produced immediately an American church that Rome governed without a state intermediary. John Carroll grasped the implication: a government that cannot establish a religion cannot suppress one either.

What the argument does not claim. It does not claim that British India policy was designed to produce any American outcome; it claims the opposite. It does not treat Catholic contribution as a hidden hand — French and Spanish intervention was public, treaty-bound and self-interested. And it does not require the Bengal famine to be read as intentional; the culpability lies in an extraction structure that continued through it.

Suppression and Restoration: A Causal Decomposition

In brief

The Society was suppressed by Catholic kings, preserved by an Orthodox empress, given political room by a man who imprisoned the pope, and restored by that pope three months after his release. Each of those four statements is documented, and together they explain 1814 without appeal to any hidden actor.

Who suppressed it

The agent was a coalition of Catholic crowns, not a doctrinal judgment. Braganza Portugal initiated in 1759; the Bourbon courts of France (1764), Spain (1767), Naples and Sicily (1767) and Parma (1768) followed, bound by the Pacte de Famille of 1761 and acting jointly from 1767. They pressed the 1769 conclave; the Spanish ambassador Floridablanca extracted the brief in 1773, with France holding Avignon and Naples holding Benevento as leverage and the threat of national schism explicit. The motive was regalist rather than theological: these governments wanted appointments, revenues and education under royal control, and the Society's fourth vow, independent colleges and Paraguay reductions made it the one body that could not be nationalized.

A note on the Order of Malta, which followed a different mechanism. Its territorial restoration was provided for at Amiens in 1802 and never carried out; Britain refused to evacuate and the 1814 Treaty of Paris confirmed Malta as British. Its institutional recovery was papal and late: Rome in 1834, the Grand Mastership in 1879. The United States played no part in either, and its use of Valletta as a naval base after 1801 amounted to accepting the dispossession. The Society survived by relocating its canonical existence; the Order survived by giving up territory and keeping sovereignty.

After 1773 the American ex-Jesuits had a headquarters, and it was in Maryland — not Boston, which had no Catholic church until 1788. Their continuity is a story about corporate law and Russian foreign policy.

The legal vehicle. The Corporation of Roman Catholic Clergymen, chartered by the Maryland legislature in 1792, took title to the estates and still exists. It is the mechanism by which a canonically non-existent order kept its property — the same manoeuvre the Order of Christ performed in reverse, and the reason the American Society had assets to restart with.

The Russian affiliation. Carroll opened correspondence with the Russian province around 1803. Rome permitted the Maryland ex-Jesuits to affiliate with the surviving Russian province; in 1805 the remaining men renewed their vows at Georgetown under Robert Molyneux, and a novitiate opened in 1806. From 1805 to 1814 the American Jesuits existed canonically as a dependency of Polotsk. An Orthodox empress's refusal to publish a papal brief is the legal thread by which the Society survived in the United States.

What the endowment was. The estates were tobacco plantations worked by enslaved people, and protecting them from confiscation is why the ex-Jesuits stayed organized at all. In 1838 the Maryland Jesuits sold 272 enslaved people to Louisiana planters, substantially to relieve Georgetown's debts. Any account of how the Society survived the suppression in America has to carry this, because the property that carried it is the same property.

The Hidden Instrument: Catholic Care, 1776 and 1938

In brief

Unconditional medical care given to religious outsiders did more to dissolve American anti-Catholicism than any argument. Two cases bracket the whole arc: a deist nursed by a Jesuit in 1776, when Maryland Catholics were barred from office, and a Protestant boy nursed by sisters in 1938, when their hospitals were part of the civic furniture of a midwestern city.

The most effective Catholic instrument in American history was not argument, money or political office. It was medical care given without condition to people who had every social reason to distrust the giver — and it worked on individuals decades before it worked on the public.

This is the Hospitaller charism, traceable to the Amalfitan hospital of the 1040s with which this timeline opens, and it is the one thing the American record shows working continuously from the Revolution to the present. Two cases bracket the arc, and the inversion between them is the finding.

The inversion is the finding. In 1784 a Protestant's recommendation determined the governance of an American Catholic institution. From 1959 forward, men formed in Catholic institutions — Creighton, the parish schools Keough was paying for on $12,000 a year — shaped the board and the deal flow of a Protestant-led company. The same mechanism of trust built through proximity, running the other way, one hundred and seventy-five years apart. Between the endpoints sit the Ursulines and Jackson at New Orleans in 1815, the cholera and yellow-fever epidemics, and the Civil War nursing that put a monument to the sisters in Washington in 1924. The care is in the record throughout; it is the telling that has been thin.

A limit on the Buffett case. Buffett has consistently attributed the 1988–93 Coca-Cola purchase to the economics of the business, and Keough was not a decision-maker in it. The defensible claim is that the friendship gave unusual access to management judgment and that Keough later served as a Berkshire director — not that the friendship caused the investment. Stated more strongly it invites the obvious rebuttal.

Piracy, Privateering, and the Silver Chain

In brief

Piracy and the opium trade are connected — through silver, as two sequential solutions to the same balance-of-payments problem, separated by fifty years. They are not connected by continuity of personnel, and the claim that pirates ran opium for the Company fails on dates.

The chain. Spanish American silver was the principal target of Caribbean piracy and privateering between roughly 1570 and 1700. The same silver flowed to Canton, because China would sell tea and take little else. The resulting bullion drain was Britain's structural problem after 1765, and Bengal opium under the 1773 monopoly was the commodity that reversed the flow, so that tea could be bought with Indian opium rather than European silver. The Ganj-i-Sawai plunder of 1695 included Spanish rials; the same metal ran through both eras.

Why the personnel claim fails. The golden age of Atlantic piracy ends around 1722–26; the Bengal opium monopoly begins in 1773. There is no overlap. The men who carried opium to China were licensed country traders and, after 1834, private firms — smugglers under Qing law and lawful merchants under British law. Calling them pirates is rhetorically satisfying and legally wrong, and the wrongness will be used against the argument.

Where the categories genuinely merge. On the Chinese coast. Opium moved inshore in the armed "fast crabs" and "scrambling dragons," crewed by men who ran contraband and raided by turn; Zheng Yi Sao's confederation levied protection over coastal shipping in the same waters, 1801–10, which is why the clippers were armed. That is a documented commercial relationship between the trade and organized maritime force — narrower than the popular claim, and much harder to dismiss.

The skull and crossbones. The Essene attribution does not hold: ossuary reburial was a general Second Temple Jewish practice, roughly 20 BC to AD 70, and the Qumran cemetery contains primary inhumations without ossuaries. The documented lineage is Christian — Adam's skull at Golgotha beneath the crucifix, and memento mori in seventeenth- and eighteenth-century funerary carving across Britain, the Low Countries and New England, which sailors saw in every churchyard. The earliest firm flag attestation, Emanuel Wynne off Santiago de Cuba in 1700, pairs skull and crossbones with an hourglass, making the design a legible sentence: your time is running out.

The American inheritance. What the colonies retained was capacity, not criminality: shipwrights, gunners, prize crews, admiralty lawyers, and merchant houses that knew how to arm a vessel and sell a cargo of uncertain provenance. In the imperial wars that capacity was licensed as privateering. In 1775 Congress licensed it again, and its navy's first operation was a raid on Nassau — the pirate republic Woodes Rogers had suppressed in 1718.

The Nest and the Port: Human Continuity from Piracy to Revolution

In brief

An earlier formulation on this page held that piracy suppression reached 1776 through law rather than through people. That was too narrow. Two major studies show the transmission also ran through the port communities themselves — the merchants, governors, prize-court officers and families who sheltered pirates in the 1690s and resisted imperial jurisdiction thereafter. Not the same individuals; the same social formation.

Piracy lived on land

Mark Hanna's Pirate Nests and the Rise of the British Empire turns the subject inside out. Rather than following colorful individuals at sea, Hanna concentrates on the nexus between land and sea, arguing that piracy persists for a reason and that when the reason disappears the pirates do too — so the supportive nests ashore, not the perpetrators afloat, are what suppression must actually target.[19] Piracy and unregulated privateering flourished in the Pacific, Caribbean and Indian Ocean because of the active support of merchant elites in the North American colonies; far from hindering trade, the marauders' enterprises contributed to commercial development and to the economic infrastructure of the port towns.[19]

The political content is explicit. Hanna argues that wherever piracy flourished, it was because landed communities had political reasons for hindering imperial attempts to define piracy as entirely criminal, and that tolerance for pirates served as a test of where colonists stood on imperial integration: one faction accepted integration as the price of prosperity, while others — notably in the non-royal colonies — preferred the immediate benefits that sheltering pirates brought to hard-pressed local societies.[19] Popular support rested on traditions of self-reliance and autonomy that rejected metropolitan attempts to control the situation, and reviewers have noted that the account helps locate early American history and the Revolution.[19]

The pirates held office

Douglas Burgess's The Politics of Piracy supplies the personnel directly. Against the unlettered stereotype, he shows that pirates and illicit traders moved easily in colonial society, attaining respectability and even political office, while the goods they supplied became a cornerstone of colonial trade and transformed port cities from barren outposts into rich capitals.[20] The transformation reached the political sphere: colonial governors furnished local mariners with privateering commissions, presided over prize courts that validated stolen wares, and defended their prerogatives as vice-admirals.[20]

His conclusion is the one this section exists to record. The war on piracy, remembered as a triumph for the English state and its colonies, in fact drove a wedge between them, imperiling the American enterprise and bringing the colonies to the verge of rebellion; by the end of the seventeenth century the social and political structures erected in the colonies to protect illicit trade amounted to an independent American legal system, and the resulting tensions presage, and may predestine, the dissolution of 1776.[20] His closing chapter is titled "Forgotten Revolutions."

The corrected formulation
Anti-piracy reached 1776 through two channels, not one. The jurisdiction persisted in statute — vice-admiralty trials without juries, enacted in 1698 and 1700 and still a grievance seventy years later. The constituency persisted in the ports — the merchant families, governors' offices, prize courts and legal structures built to shelter illicit trade, which remained in place to resist the same jurisdiction in 1765. Human continuity here means continuity of a social formation, not of individuals, and it requires no one to live eighty years.

Why this strengthens the licence thesis rather than complicating it. Hanna's reframing supplies the missing middle term. If piracy lives in the communities that service it, then suppressing piracy necessarily means asserting jurisdiction over colonial ports, colonial juries and colonial governors — which is exactly what the 1698 and 1700 acts did. The constitutional grievance of the 1760s is therefore not a coincidence of legal form but the predictable consequence of how the earlier suppression had to be carried out. The licence was policed by taking authority away from the places that issued the unofficial version of it.

What remains unproven. None of this establishes that shipboard pirate governance — elected captains, written articles, shared plunder, compensation for injury — entered American republican thought. That is a separate claim, argued by Rediker and Linebaugh, and it is graded separately at §21, Claim 11. The institutional and social transmission is documented; the ideological transmission is not, and merging the two would weaken both.

The Three Cities, Settled on Dates

In brief

The claim that London, the Vatican and Washington form one sovereign entity dissolves when their legal histories are laid side by side. They are three different legal species, created eight centuries apart, and the one alleged to be the senior partner is the one Congress can legislate for at will.

A fourth legal species, in the same city. The Order of St John is sovereign in international law with no territory, operating from extraterritorial premises in Rome — Palazzo Malta on Via dei Condotti, the Villa del Priorato on the Aventine, and since 1998 Fort St Angelo at Birgu under a 99-year agreement carrying limited extraterritoriality and no right of asylum. Its international personality is functional: recognized by the Italian Court of Cassation in 1935, exercised through diplomatic relations with more than a hundred states, its own passports, and permanent observer status at the United Nations — all without governing territory or a population. Vatican City, by contrast, is sovereign with territory and citizens. Rome therefore contains two sovereign subjects of different kinds, neither with jurisdiction over the other, which is the clearest available demonstration that "sovereign city" names a legal form rather than a rank.

The Temple Church shows what the resemblance amounts to. The Templars' English headquarters passed, after 1312, to the lawyers of the Inner and Middle Temple. The premises continued; the order did not. That is the general form of the apparent continuities in this material.

On the Carroll land. Daniel Carroll of Duddington's property was taken under the 1791 proprietors' agreement, compensated by lot sales and disputed at the time — L'Enfant demolished his house over it. It was a cession with compensation and litigation, not a donation establishing a city-state.

On the DC flag. Three stars and two bars come from the Washington family arms, documented from the fourteenth century and adopted for the flag in 1938 after a design competition. The Fibonacci reading cannot fail — the early terms cover nearly every small count anyone would use — and a claim with no falsification condition proves nothing, while discrediting the material around it.

The real asymmetry. Of the three, the District is the only one whose inhabitants lost rights at its founding and have not recovered them. The City kept its liberties through every reform; the papacy recovered sovereignty by treaty; Washingtonians were disenfranchised in 1801 to make a capital no state could coerce, and the 1783 Philadelphia mutiny remains the whole justification. That asymmetry of power is visible entirely in statute, and is more interesting than the hidden one.

Graded Claims: Documented, Contested, Refuted

In brief

Claims that circulate widely are kept here with their status, rather than dropped. Documenting folklore as folklore is itself scholarly work, and it protects the surrounding argument from being read as assertion.

Claim 01 Refuted

The American flag derives from the East India Company's

The claimThe Continental Colors of 1775 copied the Company's striped flag, and Franklin urged its adoption in a speech at a Philadelphia dinner.
What failsFranklin was not on the Naval Committee and had no known role in the design; the flag-committee story is a myth. Fawcett, who advanced the derivation thesis in 1937, could not trace the speech and said so; its date of 13 December falls ten days after the Colors were raised aboard the Alfred, and the delegates were in Philadelphia, not Cambridge. Company ships wore the striped colours only east of the Cape, flying the red ensign in Atlantic waters.[4]
What remainsA real visual resemblance, best explained as convergence within a shared maritime design vocabulary, and a useful specimen of citation decay.
Claim 02 Refuted

The skull and crossbones descends from Essene ossuary practice

What failsOssuary reburial was general in Second Temple Judea, not an Essene distinctive, and the Qumran cemetery — the site most associated with the Essenes — contains primary inhumations without ossuaries.
What remainsA documented Christian lineage: Adam's skull at Golgotha in crucifixion iconography, and memento mori funerary carving across northern Europe and New England. Wynne's 1700 flag pairs the device with an hourglass, completing the sentence.
Claim 03 Refuted

Private families bought the East India Company

What failsThe Company was wound up by statute — monopolies stripped in 1813 and 1833, trading ceased 1834, dissolved 1874. No transfer of the corporation to private hands occurred.
What remains, and is strongerThe private houses captured the deregulated trade after 1834 — Jardine Matheson, Dent, Russell & Co, the Bombay Parsi and Baghdadi houses. That is documented and much harder to argue with than an ownership transfer refutable in one sentence.
Claim 04 Refuted

London, the Vatican and Washington form one sovereign entity

What failsThree legal species created eight centuries apart; see §17. The supposed senior partner is the one Congress can legislate for at will. The Carroll land was a compensated cession, not a donation; the DC flag's three stars and two bars come from the Washington arms.
What remainsA genuine asymmetry — the District's residents lost rights at its founding and have not recovered them — visible entirely in statute.
Claim 05 Refuted

A Templar fleet carried the order's assets to Portugal and thence to the Atlantic

What failsThe Order of Christ was a royal creation of 1319 holding Templar property under crown authority, made a perpetual crown administration in 1523. Prince Henry's voyages were funded by its revenues. The documented mechanism requires no surviving navy.
What remainsA real institutional continuity of assets and personnel in Iberia — which is what Ad Providam failed to transfer there, and which is more interesting than a fleet.
Claim 06 Refuted

American influence restored the Jesuits; Carroll caused 1814

What failsThe decisive causes were European: the collapse of the Bourbon veto, the Russian survival, and Pius VII's captivity. America was the fourth and last of the partial restorations, after Russia (1801) and Naples (1804). Rome had already erected Baltimore as a metropolitan see in 1808 — investing in America six years before the restoration, which is causation running the other way.
What remains, and is betterCarroll caused the American province; and the United States gave a suppressed order a place to hold 12,000 acres and run a college in the open while Catholic Europe still forbade it. That is the same mechanism as the Franklin case: an absence of state authority over religion producing consequences no one designed.
Claim 07 Not used

Napoleon as initiated Templar, and Templar agency behind 1814

The claimNapoleon held an esoteric initiation, went to Rome in search of missing Templar records, and thereby caused the Jesuit restoration.
Why the inference fails even if the premise is grantedThe restoration followed his defeat: Pius VII returned in May 1814 and signed in August, three months after Napoleon's power collapsed. His ecclesiastical policy — the Concordat, the Organic Articles, the annexation of the Papal States — was regalist, the very program a fourth vow to the pope opposes. And the necessary condition, the survival at Polotsk from 1773, predates him and lay in an empire he invaded and failed to hold.
The usable residueAn irony, not an agency: the Napoleonic wars destroyed the regalist state system that had suppressed the Society, and the man who imprisoned the pope made the Society's restoration possible by failing.
Claim 08 Contested — usable in a narrowed form

Pirates worked for the East India Company

What failsDates: Atlantic piracy ends c.1722–26, the opium monopoly begins 1773. And the Every episode cuts directly against it — the Company lost five factories and its officers' liberty to a pirate's act.
What survives, and is strongPiracy and chartered monopoly were two licensing states of one armed commercial system, and the boundary was drawn by whoever held the charter. Supporting: Child's War, in which the Company did under charter what Every was hanged for; the interloper, Angre and Qawasim cases, in which the label removed commercial rivals; the productivity of the threat, which justified armed Indiamen, convoys, extraterritorial jurisdiction and the Bombay Marine; the armed inshore distribution network on the China coast; and the absorptive amnesties of 1698 and 1717.
Weaker arguments, droppedThat the Company's disavowal was merely interested testimony — its Surat losses were real and a defence under duress can still be true. That Qing illegality makes the opium traders pirates — smuggling with escorts is still not taking other ships' cargo by force.
Claim 09 Contested

Opium capital funded Skull and Bones

StatusWilliam Huntington Russell, who founded the society at Yale in 1832, was a cousin of Samuel Russell of Russell & Co. Treat the kinship as fact and the funding as asserted rather than documented. The related Yale link — Elihu Yale's Madras fortune, made amid Company corruption proceedings — is institutional descent through an endowment, not continuity of conduct.
Claim 10 Contested

Financial engineering lay behind the Jesuit suppression

StatusThe documented drivers are the Bourbon courts and Pombal, acting on regalist and colonial grounds, with the reductions and the Society's independent revenues as the grievance. A banking explanation is not needed and is not evidenced. Similarly, Rothschild involvement in papal finance is real for the nineteenth century (from the 1832 loan) and absent from the IOR, founded in 1942 under Nogara; conflating the two periods is the standard error.
Claim 11 Contested — two schools

Caribbean pirate governance shaped American political development

For, ideologicalRediker's Villains of All Nations and Between the Devil and the Deep Blue Sea, and Linebaugh and Rediker's The Many-Headed Hydra, argue for a revolutionary Atlantic in which a multiracial maritime workforce carried radical practice — elected captains, written articles, shared plunder, compensation for injury — into the Age of Revolution.[22][23]
For, institutional and socialHanna and Burgess argue, from different premises, that the communities which sheltered pirates built the colonial legal and political structures that later refused imperial authority, and that pirates and illicit traders held respectable standing and public office.[19][20] See §19.
AgainstNo founder cites pirate articles. The shipboard practices have closer analogues in ordinary merchant-ship custom and in earlier buccaneer chasse-partie agreements. And the colonial elite prosecuted pirates readily once imperial integration served its interests — the same men on both sides of the transition.
AssessmentThe institutional and social transmission is documented and belongs on the page. The ideological transmission of shipboard democracy into republican thought remains the weaker claim. The two should be argued separately: merging them lets the stronger carry the weaker, which is how the composite version gets dismissed whole.
Framing safeguard
The Baghdadi strand is real history and belongs in the record — but it travels with the Scottish and New England houses at equal weight, which the evidence supports, and with the British-state monopoly that created the trade named first. Keeping all three in frame is both accurate and what protects the argument from being read as something it is not.

Conclusion

Three observations survive the sorting.

The category was always legal, never moral. Every was hanged for what Child's Company had done under charter. The Qawasim were destroyed for taxing a route the Company taxed. When the colonists became a state, their first act at sea was to issue commissions of their own. Nothing in this requires a hidden hand; it requires only that one notice who holds the pen.

Anti-piracy built the machinery that provoked the Revolution, and left the constituency to resist it. Vice-admiralty jurisdiction without juries entered American life in 1698 as a Company remedy against pirate-harbouring ports. Seventy years later it was the grievance. The transmission ran on two channels: the jurisdiction persisted in statute, and the port communities that had sheltered the trade — their merchant families, governors' offices and prize courts — persisted alongside it. The empire's instrument for policing its own frontier of licence became the instrument that lost it the frontier, in the same places it had first been aimed.

Institutions are rarely restored by their friends. The Society was suppressed by Catholic kings, preserved by an Orthodox empress, given political room by a man who imprisoned the pope, and restored by that pope three months after his release. The Order of Malta was rescued uncanonically by a Russian emperor, lost its island to a British refusal to evacuate, and recovered as sovereignty exercised from two extraterritorial houses in Rome rather than over any territory of its own. The papacy spent fifty-nine years with no land at all. And the one body here that was never suppressed — the Company, which had no principle beyond extraction — was wound up by statute and left no successor.

Two dates are worth noticing now that everything sits in one sequence. 21 July and 16 December 1773 fall five months apart: the Society suppressed under crown pressure, and the Company's surplus destroyed in Boston — the two institutions that had carried European expansion into Asia and America, both broken in the same year by the states that had chartered them. And 1784 does double duty: in February the Empress of China sails to breach the Company's monopoly; in May Congress disclaims authority over religion; in June a suppressed Jesuit is named to lead the American church on a Protestant deist's recommendation; and in August Parliament puts the Company under a Board of Control. The new republic took up one licensing power and set down another in the same six months.

Summary
Licence, not conspiracy. The institutions that survived suppression did so by moving their licence to a jurisdiction that would not enforce it; the institution that held the most licence and the least principle is the only one that ended.
