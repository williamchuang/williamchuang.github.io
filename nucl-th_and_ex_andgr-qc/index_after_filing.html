Logarchéon and Nuclear Physics: Evaluation and Funding Routes

Logarchéon's AI designs draw on statistical physics, hyperbolic geometry and causal inference, the same mathematics used in nuclear physics, fusion and gravitation. Some of the links are exact identities; others are analogies that still require testing. Each link is graded, followed by the national-security frame and the funding routes.

William Huanshan Chuang (莊桓山) · Logarchéon Inc.

Terms

  • Critical-entropy attention (CEAS): sets the "temperature" of each attention unit so its output stays near a phase transition, where it is most sensitive.
  • Ψ causal engine: answers cause-and-effect questions from data, and states when the data cannot decide (abstention).
  • GRAIL and MIA: attention computed from distances in hyperbolic space, so results are unchanged under that space's symmetries, including Lorentz boosts.
  • Two-phase scheme: methods learned on small models are applied to a large model by changing only a small set of control parameters.
  • Twin map; DL ≠ LD: a change of parameters that leaves a model's results unchanged; training does not commute with it ("DL ≠ LD"), so applying it changes how training proceeds.
  • Trace store; gate: a store that cross-checks spectra through trace identities, and a statistical test that accepts a change only when its benefit is bounded with stated confidence.

Standard of evidence

A link is listed only when a Logarchéon mechanism has a named counterpart in physics. Results proved or measured in Logarchéon's tests count for more than analogies. The work stays within open, unclassified science: nuclear structure and reactions, nuclear data, emulators, collision data and nonproliferation analytics.

Summary of findings

The overlap with nuclear physics is real but narrower than "almost everything". Several of these tools came from nuclear physics originally: random-matrix theory, for example, was first used for nuclear energy levels. The most promising contributions are those already proved or measured:

  1. DL ≠ LD. Symmetry-generated transformations and preconditioning, with a proved bound on error amplification.
  2. Certified trace checks. The trace store as a check of semiclassical shell structure.
  3. Identification with abstention. The causal engine for nuclear data evaluation.
  4. Gated, traceable updates. The gate and hash chain as verification and provenance.
  5. Exact Lorentz invariance. GRAIL/MIA for collision data, against the existing Lorentz-equivariant networks (LorentzNet, PELICAN).
  6. Compression. Large models on one processor, on site.

DL ≠ LD: tunnelling in the Logarchéon sense

In Logarchéon, tunnelling is the twin jump and its non-commutation with training. Let D be a gradient step and L a twin map, an invertible map with ℓ(Lθ) = ℓ(θ). The following identity is proved:

D(Lθ) − L·D(θ) = η (L − L−⊤) ∇ℓ(θ)

The commutator vanishes exactly when L is orthogonal; for a Lorentz boost of rapidity φ its size is 2 sinh φ. A twin jump reaches a point of equal loss at no cost, and training continues from there under the preconditioner (LᵀL)⁻¹. Mapped back, a boost amplifies error by at most eφ.

Three nuclear counterparts:

  1. Transforming before an approximate solve. Ab initio theory applies transformations that keep exact energies but speed the truncated calculation. Unitary ones are the orthogonal case; non-unitary ones are the boosts. The bound eφ is the norm of the inverse map, which bounds error growth under any similarity transformation; what the designs add is the choice of the transformation through the gate. Bosonic Bogoliubov transformations have the boost's form, u = cosh r, v = sinh r.
  2. Variational wavefunctions for light nuclei. Neural-network wavefunctions need a good preconditioner (stochastic reconfiguration, whose metric depends on the parameters). A twin jump is a fixed preconditioner change obtained from a symmetry.
  3. Fission paths. The action S = ∫√(2M(q)(V − E)) ds does not depend on the shape coordinates, but gradient-based path searches do unless they use the inertia tensor M as metric. The covariant transport rule is that requirement; M plays the role of LᵀL, by analogy, since M varies along the path.

Status. The commutator is classical: it changes how descent moves, not barrier penetration with amplitude e−S. On small character models, twin jumps changed the final loss by under 0.001. The claim to test is symmetry-generated preconditioning or transformation with a proved bound.

From language to nuclei: what each stage means

In a language model the units are words. Here the units are nuclei: each token is a nucleus (proton number Z, neutron number N), one of its energy levels, or a reaction. The stages then read as follows.

  1. Data. Measured masses, levels and cross sections become the sequence the model reads, in place of text.
  2. Geometry (GRAIL). Every token and every parameter is a point in hyperbolic space, and the model's scores are distances between points. Training moves points, so the loss is a landscape over that geometry. With a discrete group Γ, it is a landscape on the quotient Γ\ℍⁿ, a finite space with the symmetries built in.
  3. Twin maps (Lorentz group). Moving all points by the same symmetry leaves every prediction unchanged. This is like changing the basis or coordinates in a nuclear calculation: the physics is the same, but how the calculation converges is not (DL ≠ LD).
  4. Laplace eigenfunctions: the normal modes of the landscape. Any function on the landscape, the loss included, can be written as a sum of the geometry's normal modes, as a vibration is written as a sum of harmonics. The eigenvalue says how fast a mode varies. Low modes carry broad structure across the whole dataset; high modes carry local detail. For nuclear data, the hypothesis is that smooth trends across the chart of nuclides fall in the low modes and shell effects in higher ones. This is untested.
  5. Hecke operators: extra labels of the model's modes. When Γ is arithmetic, a Hecke operator T_p averages each point with p + 1 related points of the geometry and commutes with the Laplacian, so its eigenvalues can tell apart modes that the Laplacian alone does not. These are labels of the model's geometry, not nuclear quantum numbers: the nucleus has no Hecke operators, and arithmetic spectra have Poisson level spacings while nuclear spectra have GOE spacings.
  6. Spectral fitting. The labelled modes give a kernel basis in which a model of nuclear data could be fitted mode by mode. The basis is not computed from arithmetic counts alone: Maass eigenvalues are found numerically, and recovering them from the length spectrum needs exponentially many geodesics. The loss is not linear in the mode coefficients, so the fit does not replace gradient steps. This stage is untested.
  7. Two phases. Modes and programs found on light nuclei, where exact calculations exist, are applied to heavy nuclei by changing only a small set of control parameters.

Status on a 32-billion-parameter model. Stages 1–3 are built and tested on language data. On an open-weight language model of 32.8 billion parameters, held at 2.6 bits per weight in 11.8 GB of memory:

  • all 4,096 attention heads were lifted into hyperbolic geometry with no retraining, at a cost of 0.65% or less in perplexity up to moderate curvature (16.84 → 16.79–16.95);
  • at the strongest curvature tested, perplexity rose to 40.17, and 60 steps of training on the control set brought it back to 18.14; the statistical gate has not yet accepted this, and nearly all of the remaining cost lies in the early layers;
  • the coset matrices of the Hecke operators T₂ and T₃ ran on six lifted heads at a cost of about 0.2% before any training; no automorphic kernel was formed, so they did not act as Hecke operators on the model;
  • the CEAS temperature program, accepted by the gate, recovered about 5% of the loss from compression, with its target at the critical point T = 1 rather than the design's T* = 1 − u_min, and a fact update through an adapter passed checks on unrelated text and on 200 general-knowledge questions.

Stages 4–7 on nuclear data are untested. Hecke operators exist only when Γ is arithmetic.

Gravitation (gr-qc)

Gravity in 2+1 dimensions (AdS₃, whose Euclidean form is hyperbolic 3-space) gives exact links, including the Hecke side that has no nuclear use. Logarchéon works with the Lorentz group O(n−1,1) in any dimension, so the image-sum and trace-formula links extend to higher-dimensional AdS quotients and to 3+1 cosmology; the Hecke links are specific to AdS₃.

A dimensional map: what each parameter measures in nuclei

Planck's constant entered physics in 1900 as a fitting parameter in the blackbody law. The relation it sat in, E = hν, fixed its dimension as energy × time, that of action or angular momentum, before its meaning was understood; Bohr quantised angular momentum in units of h/2π in 1913. Dimension analysis does the same work here in a limited form: placed in a nuclear relation, a parameter of a Logarchéon construct acquires a dimension, and the dimension names the known quantity, a temperature, a mass, an energy or a label, against which it is measured. The analysis decides what to measure; it does not decide that a parameter is a new constant. The table at the end of the section gives each parameter's status: known physics, set by construction, fitted, or a quantity of the model.

Definitions

  1. Units. Natural units ħ = c = kB = 1. Every dimension is a power of energy, [X] = MeVd, with lengths converted by ħc = 197.327 MeV·fm.
  2. Construct. A Logarchéon construct is a map with parameters: the Gibbs selector pj(β) = eβsj/Z, the gap score T, the lifted score with curvature c, the Laplacian ΔΓ and Hecke operators Tp on Γ\ℍⁿ, the twin map L and the gradient step D(θ) = θ − η∇ℓ.
  3. Dictionary. A dictionary ι assigns to each model variable a physical quantity: scores to minus energies, sj ↦ −Ej; query and key vectors, which the lift treats as tangent vectors, to spatial momenta, with |q| = mζ for rapidity ζ; the loss to a variational energy.
  4. Acquired dimension. The dimension a parameter must carry for every relation containing it to be dimensionally homogeneous under ι.
  5. Candidate scale. A parameter whose acquired dimension is non-zero.
  6. Physical constant. A candidate scale whose fitted value is universal: equal, within stated uncertainty, across independent systems and observables. Planck's h met this test: one value fitted blackbody spectra (1900) and the photoelectric effect (1905).

Postulates

  1. Homogeneity. Every argument of exp, log, sinh, cosh and of a softmax is dimensionless; both sides of every equation carry the same dimension.
  2. Fidelity of the dictionary. Under ι, a construct computes the physical quantity it is mapped to; for example the Gibbs selector computes Boltzmann occupation probabilities.
  3. Empirical decision. Dimension analysis fixes dimensions only. Whether a candidate scale is a physical constant is decided by the universality test of Definition 6, never by the analysis itself.

Common notion. Quantities equal to the same quantity carry the same dimension.

Propositions: the dimensions the constructs acquire

Proposition 1 (inverse temperature). Under sj ↦ −Ej, [β] = MeV⁻¹, and 1/β is a nuclear temperature.

Proof. The selector contains e−βEj; by Postulate 1 the exponent βEj is dimensionless, so [β][E] = 1. ∎

Proposition 2 (the gap score is a heat-capacity ratio). The CEAS design fixes the critical inverse temperature of a head at βc = √(2 ln N)/σ, from the quadratic expansion of the entropy and the extreme-value gap σ√(2 ln N), and scores a head by T = β/βc. Let a nuclear model space of N states have a Gaussian density of states of centroid Ē and width σ, as finite shell-model spaces have to good approximation (French–Wong). Then, while the mean energy lies above the ground-state edge,

Smax − S(β) = C(β)/2, T² = β²σ²/(2 ln N) = C/(2Smax) = 1 − S/Smax

with Smax = ln N and heat capacity C = β² Var(E). T = 1 occurs when the mean energy reaches Ē − σ√(2 ln N), the expected lowest of N independent Gaussian levels to leading order in ln N, at the temperature

Tf = σ / √(2 ln N)

Proof. ln Z = ln N − βĒ + β²σ²/2, so ⟨E⟩ = Ē − βσ², Var(E) = σ² and C = β²σ². Then S = ln Z + β⟨E⟩ = ln N − β²σ²/2 = Smax − C/2; divide by Smax = ln N. Setting ⟨E⟩ = Ē − σ√(2 ln N) gives β = √(2 ln N)/σ, that is T = 1. ∎

Numbers. For ²⁸Si in the sd shell, N = 93,710 (M-scheme), ln N = 11.448 and Tf = 0.209 σ; at this N the extreme-value correction to the leading order is about 9%. Tf depends on the chosen model space through N and σ, so it is a property of the truncation, not of the nucleus alone.

Relation to known physics. The calculation is the annealed entropy of the random energy model (Derrida, 1981), and Ē − σ√(2 ln N) is the Gaussian ground-state estimate of statistical spectroscopy (French–Wong; Ratcliff). The CEAS design reaches the same threshold from its own premises. Real shell-model levels are correlated (GOE), and a Gaussian density has a heat capacity C = σ²/T² that falls smoothly, with no pairing peak; the comparison with the pairing temperature Tc = 0.567 Δ is therefore a question about a fixed model space, not a statement about measured heat capacities.

Proposition 3 (curvature acquires a mass). Under the dictionary that maps query and key vectors to spatial momenta, [c] = MeV⁻² and Λ = c−1/2 is a mass. Identifying the lifted hyperboloid with the mass shell sets c = 1/m², so Λ = m by construction; and since the lift places a vector q at geodesic distance |q| from the origin, |q|/m is the rapidity ζ, and the dimensionless curvature of the K9 sweep, averaged over queries and keys, is

κ = c⟨|q|²⟩ = ⟨ζ²⟩

Proof. The lifted score contains sinh(√c |q|), so [c][q]² = 1 by Postulate 1. The mass shell is a hyperboloid of radius m in Minkowski momentum space, of sectional curvature −1/m²; identifying the two gives c = 1/m². The exponential map at the origin sends q to the point at distance |q|, whose spatial coordinate is m sinh(|q|/m) = |p|, so |q|/m = ζ. ∎

Numbers. κ = 0.1, 0.3 and 1 correspond to typical speeds v = tanh √κ = 0.306, 0.499 and 0.762. The heads have dimension 128, so the lift lives in ℍ¹²⁸; the reading as a mass shell, which is ℍ³, holds only for three-dimensional inputs.

Proposition 4 (the boost twin is relativistic). For a boost twin of rapidity φ, the commutator size and the acceptance bound are

||L − L−⊤|| = 2 sinh φ = 2|p|/m = 2γv, eφ = √((1 + v)/(1 − v))

the form of the relativistic Doppler factor. The boost acts on the parameters, not on a particle, so the reading of φ as a rapidity is an analogy; the bound eφ = ‖L⁻¹‖ holds for any non-unitary similarity transformation.

Proof. sinh φ = γv and eφ = γ(1 + v) = √((1 + v)/(1 − v)) for v = tanh φ. ∎

Numbers. φ = 0.2 gives v = 0.197 and eφ = 1.221.

Proposition 5 (a twin step is imaginary-time evolution). For any invertible linear L, gradient descent with step η in the coordinates φ = Lθ, on ℓ′(φ) = ℓ(L⁻¹φ), equals

θ ↦ θ − η (LᵀL)⁻¹ ∇ℓ(θ)

If ℓ is a variational energy E[θ] (MeV) and θ is dimensionless, then [η] = MeV⁻¹: the step is an interval of imaginary time. With the quantum geometric tensor G in place of LᵀL, the same rule is stochastic reconfiguration, imaginary-time evolution e−ηH projected onto the variational manifold (Sorella; Carleo–Troyer); G depends on θ and is not of the form LᵀL for a twin map, so this case is metric preconditioning in general, not a twin step.

Proof. ∇φℓ′ = L−⊤∇ℓ; then φ′ = φ − ηL−⊤∇ℓ and θ′ = L⁻¹φ′ = θ − η(LᵀL)⁻¹∇ℓ. The dimension follows from [θ] = [η][∇ℓ] = [η] MeV. ∎

Numbers. Excited admixtures decay as e−E₁τ after imaginary time τ = Kη, E₁ being the lowest excitation of the same quantum numbers. For ⁴He the lowest 0⁺ excitation is the p + ³H threshold at 19.81 MeV; suppression by e−10 needs τ = 0.505 MeV⁻¹ of imaginary time. A twin map changes the number of steps K needed at fixed accuracy; K is the measured quantity.

Proposition 6 (closed geodesics acquire the dimension of time). In the Selberg trace formula of a compact hyperbolic surface (for the non-compact PSL(2,ℤ)\ℍ cusp and scattering terms are added)

Σj h(rj) = (Area/4π) ∫ r h(r) tanh(πr) dr + Σγ ℓ(γ₀) g(ℓ(γ)) / (2 sinh(ℓ(γ)/2)), g(u) = (1/2π) ∫ h(r) e−iru dr

the product rℓ is dimensionless. Under r ↦ E/ε₀, a closed geodesic of length ℓγ is an orbit of period tγ = ℓγ/ε₀ and produces an oscillation of the level density with period

ΔEγ = 2π ε₀ / ℓγ

Proof. g enters through e−irℓ, so [r][ℓ] = 1 (Postulate 1). In Gutzwiller's formula an orbit of period T contributes cos(S(E)/ħ − μπ/2) with dS/dE = T, whose period in energy is 2πħ/T. ∎

Numbers. The gross-shell spacing is ħω ≈ 41 A−1/3 MeV. For Γ = PSL(2,ℤ) the shortest closed geodesic has length ℓ₁ = 2 arccosh(3/2) = 1.9248, so matching ΔE₁ = ħω fixes ε₀ = ħω ℓ₁/2π = 12.56 A−1/3 MeV; for ²⁰⁸Pb, ħω = 6.92 MeV and ε₀ = 2.12 MeV. The agreement with ħω holds by construction, A−1/3 included. The independent test is the next geodesic, of trace 4 and length 2 arccosh 2 = 2.634, which predicts a second oscillation at ℓ₁/ℓ₂ = 0.73 ħω that shell data do not show. The construction is the periodic-orbit theory of shell structure (Balian–Bloch; Gutzwiller; Strutinsky) with the orbits of a chosen surface; the modular surface has chaotic, isolated orbits, while nuclear shells arise from families of regular orbits.

Proposition 7 (the number of discrete modes). For the modular surface, of area π/3, the number of Laplace eigenvalues below λ is N(λ) ~ λ/12 (Weyl–Selberg). A kernel regression on the discrete modes up to λmax therefore has about λmax/12 features per channel; 60 modes need λmax ≈ 720. A complete basis also needs the continuous (Eisenstein) part of the spectrum.

Proof. Weyl's law N(λ) ~ Area·λ/4π with Area = π/3. ∎

Numbers. A liquid-drop mass formula has 5 parameters and global mass models tens; measured masses number a few thousand. No map from the chart of nuclides to the modular surface is defined, so the count gives the size of a feature set, not parameters of nuclei.

Proposition 8 (Hecke eigenvalues are dimensionless labels of the model). The Hecke operators Tp, normalised by 1/√p, are dimensionless and satisfy [Tp, ΔΓ] = 0 and [Tp, Tq] = 0. Their joint eigenfunctions with ΔΓ are determined by the eigenvalues (λp) (strong multiplicity one), and |λp| ≤ p7/64 + p−7/64 (Kim–Sarnak).

Proof. An average over p + 1 points carries no units. Commutation and multiplicity one are the cited theorems. ∎

Consequence. The Hecke eigenvalues label modes of the model's own geometry, which the Laplace eigenvalue alone may not separate; the simplicity of the Laplace spectrum is conjectural for PSL(2,ℤ) (Cartier). They are not nuclear quantum numbers: nuclear labels such as parity and isospin projection take finitely many values and come from symmetry groups of the nucleus, which has no Hecke operators; and the Hecke structure is what gives arithmetic surfaces Poisson level spacings, while nuclear levels follow GOE spacings. Hecke operators exist only when Γ is arithmetic; the Schottky groups of the GRAIL builds are not.

Proposition 9 (transfer rule from light to heavy nuclei). A move recorded as a target gap score T* is realised in a nucleus A by βA = T*√(2 ln NA)/σA.

Proof. Solve T* = βσ/√(2 ln N) for β. ∎

Numbers. N counts the states in an energy window. With the Fermi-gas density ρ = √π e2√(aE*)/(12 a1/4 E*5/4), a ≈ A/8 MeV⁻¹, E* = 8 MeV and a window of 1 MeV: ln N = 3.3 for ¹⁶O and 23.5 for ²⁰⁸Pb, and at equal σ the same T* needs a β 2.7 times larger in ²⁰⁸Pb; keeping the exponent alone gives 8.0, 28.8 and a factor 1.9. The rule is the definition of T solved for β; it assumes equal σ and independent levels.

Proposition 10 (conditional: the depth law is a running exponent). If the layer index ℓ corresponds to renormalisation-group time t = ℓΔt = ln(μ₀/μ), with Δt fixed independently of the fit, the depth law βℓ = β₀ bαℓ becomes β(μ) = β₀(μ₀/μ)γRG with γRG = α ln b/Δt, the scaling exponent of the temperature coupling (an eigenvalue of the flow, not an anomalous dimension, which belongs to fields and operators). The depth law itself is an unproved ansatz, and without an independent Δt the exponent cannot be measured.

Proof. Substitute ℓ = t/Δt. ∎

New physics from DL ≠ LD

Proposition 14 (a twin cannot change the gap; it converts amplitude into imaginary time). Let ℓ be invariant under an invertible linear twin L that fixes a minimum θ*, and let H be the Hessian there. Then LᵀHL = H, and the twin-preconditioned step is similar to the plain step:

I − η(LᵀL)⁻¹H = L⁻¹(I − ηH)L

The spectrum, and with it the slowest rate of convergence, is unchanged. With η an interval of imaginary time (Proposition 5), the slowest rate is the gap E₁, and asymptotically the twin saves the imaginary time

Δτ = ln|a/a′| / E₁

where a and a′ are the amplitudes of the slowest mode before and after the jump.

Proof. Differentiating ℓ(Lθ) = ℓ(θ) twice at θ* gives LᵀHL = H, so L−⊤H = HL and (LᵀL)⁻¹H = L⁻¹L−⊤H = L⁻¹HL. Near θ* the error evolves as (I − ηH)^k; its slowest component decays as (1 − ηE₁)^k ≈ e−E₁τ with τ = kη, and the jump multiplies that component's amplitude by a′/a. ∎

Planck reading. The twin turns a dimensionless log-amplitude into imaginary time, and the conversion constant is 1/E₁, the gap of the nucleus: for ⁴He, 1/E₁ = 0.0505 MeV⁻¹ per unit of ln|a/a′|. At an invariant minimum DL ≠ LD has no lasting effect; a lasting gain needs either distance from the minimum or a truncated problem, in which the twin is no longer a symmetry.

Proposition 15 (the twin orbit bounds the truncation error). Let a set of twins {Li} leave the exact energy E* unchanged while a truncated solve gives Ei in frame Li. Then

maxi |Ei − E*| ≥ ½ (maxi Ei − mini Ei)

and, when the truncated solve is variational (Ei ≥ E*), maxi (Ei − E*) ≥ maxi Ei − mini Ei.

Proof. For any two frames, |Ea − Eb| ≤ |Ea − E*| + |Eb − E*| ≤ 2 maxi |Ei − E*|. In the variational case Emax − E* ≥ Emax − Emin, since Emin ≥ E*. ∎

Numbers. A Woods–Saxon well solved in a truncated basis of 8 oscillator states, with the oscillator length varied over a factor of 3 (oscillator units): for the lowest three levels the half spreads are 0.31, 1.67 and 6.76 against largest errors 0.62, 3.35 and 13.52, so the variational form of the bound holds with equality to two digits.

Reading. The bound needs no exact solution: the frame dependence of a truncated nuclear calculation is a certified lower bound on its truncation error. The same holds for the poloidal angle of a stellarator surface, a twin of the exact surface but not of its truncated Fourier series. Dependence on the flow parameter of the similarity renormalisation group is used as a diagnostic in the same spirit (Jurgenson–Navrátil–Furnstahl, 2009); the bound and the gated orbit are the designs' addition.

Candidate regularity (optimal twin rapidity). The gate selects a twin rapidity φ*, a dimensionless number. Under the boost reading of Proposition 4 its nuclear comparison is the Fermi rapidity ζF = arsinh(pF/m): 0.276 in symmetric matter and 0.346 in neutron matter at saturation density. Candidate: the ratio φ*/ζF is the same across nuclei, which would tie the best computational frame to the Fermi motion. It is decided by computation on variational light nuclei.

When an acquired scale would be new physics

Proposition 11 (classification). Every parameter of the constructs falls in exactly one of three classes under ι:

  1. a dimensionless label or indicator (T, κ, φ, λp, γRG);
  2. an algorithmic quantity, fixed by the procedure and not by nature (η, the number of steps K, the tolerance of the gate);
  3. a candidate scale of non-zero dimension (1/β, Tf, Λ, ε₀).

Universality across independent data is necessary for a parameter of class 3, or a value of class 1, to be a new constant, and not sufficient: a universal value that equals a known constant, as Λ = m does, is not new. Class 2 never is.

Proof. The classes partition the parameters by dimension and by whether the value is set by the data (Propositions 1–10). Postulate 3 makes universality the deciding condition. An algorithmic quantity changes with the procedure for the same data, so it cannot be universal across procedures. ∎

The h of 1900 belonged to class 3: a fitted parameter of non-zero dimension whose value proved the same across independent phenomena.

Other fields that share the mathematics

The mathematics of Logarchéon is not specific to nuclear physics. Five fields share several of its tools at once; eight more share one or two.

Fields that share several tools

  1. Protein folding and biophysics. Folding theory rests on the random energy model (Bryngelson–Wolynes, 1987), whose freezing point coincides with the critical point of CEAS. Its glass temperature corresponds to the Tf of the dimensional map, and the gap score is the ratio Tg/Tphys of glass temperature to physical temperature (Proposition 13). Energy landscapes, inverse design of sequences for a target fold, and causal inference apply as well.
  2. Condensed matter on hyperbolic lattices. Hyperbolic lattices have been built in circuit quantum electrodynamics (Kollár et al., 2019); their band theory uses Fuchsian groups and automorphic functions (Maciejko–Rayan, 2021), the geometry of GRAIL. The Selberg trace formula applies, and for arithmetic lattices so do Hecke operators. Spin glasses, Ising models, Lee–Yang zeros and Anderson localisation are standard tools of the field.
  3. Quantum chaos, black holes and quantum information. Random-matrix spacing statistics and spectral form factors; the SYK model of chaotic many-body physics; the JT-gravity matrix model; modular sums and Hecke operators in AdS₃; and quantum error-correcting codes on hyperbolic surfaces, built as quotients of hyperbolic space by Fuchsian groups (Breuckmann–Terhal, 2016).
  4. Particle physics and QCD. The spectrum of the QCD Dirac operator follows chiral random-matrix theory (Shuryak–Verbaarschot, 1993); collision analysis uses Lorentz-equivariant networks; the sign problem of lattice QCD is attacked by contour deformations, nonlinear changes of variables analogous to twin maps; the renormalisation group runs throughout.
  5. Quantum chemistry. Similarity transformations before an approximate solve (transcorrelated and coupled-cluster methods), which is DL ≠ LD; neural-network wavefunctions for molecules trained with natural-gradient preconditioning; level statistics of molecular spectra.

Proposition 13 (protein folding). In the random-energy model of folding, a protein has N = eS₀ misfolded configurations with Gaussian energies of width ΔE. Its glass temperature is the freezing temperature of Proposition 2, and the gap score is the ratio of the glass temperature to the temperature:

Tg = ΔE/√(2S₀), T = βΔE/√(2S₀) = Tg/Tphys

Proof. Proposition 2 with σ = ΔE and ln N = S₀ gives the freezing temperature σ/√(2 ln N) = ΔE/√(2S₀), which is the glass temperature of the random energy model (Derrida; Bryngelson–Wolynes). Then βσ/√(2 ln N) = Tg/Tphys with Tphys = 1/β. ∎

Numbers. Sequences that fold well have a high ratio of folding temperature to glass temperature, estimated at about 1.6 for small proteins (Onuchic et al., 1995). At the folding temperature the gap score is therefore about 1/1.6 ≈ 0.63: below 1, so the physical temperature lies above the glass temperature and the protein is not frozen; a gap score of 1 marks the glass transition. The proposition restates the random energy model of folding in the coordinates of the gap score; the content is known (Bryngelson–Wolynes, 1987).

National security within open science

The national-security value lies in open lanes that do not need a clearance:

  • uncertainty quantification of nuclear data, on which reactors and national programmes depend (the nuclear-data working group is interagency);
  • nonproliferation detection analytics and safeguards;
  • nuclear forensics attribution, where identification with abstention fits;
  • trustworthy, verified AI: gated, traceable updates.

Boundary. Open nuclear-structure and reaction data are unrestricted. Technical assistance on reactors or enrichment can fall under 10 CFR 810 and export controls; weapons-related work is classified. Work stays on open data, and a national-laboratory partner advises on any boundary.

First experiments

Each experiment is preregistered, uses public data and is compared with published baselines.

  1. Twin jump in variational training of a light nucleus (for example ⁴He with a neural-network wavefunction). One GPU. Tests DL ≠ LD directly; the strongest preliminary result for a proposal.
  2. Ψ abstention on optical-model fits to public cross sections (EXFOR).
  3. Trace-formula check of shell corrections against exact spectra.
  4. Spacing statistics on evaluated level schemes (ENSDF), with the gate deciding flags.
  5. Lorentz-exact classifier on simulated Electron-Ion Collider events, against LorentzNet and PELICAN.

Results will be posted on arXiv (nucl-th, gr-qc) with code, and used as preliminary results for a DOE Phase I proposal with a national-laboratory co-investigator.
