Logarchéon and Nuclear Physics: Evaluation and Funding Routes

Logarchéon's AI designs draw on statistical physics, hyperbolic geometry and causal inference, the same mathematics used in nuclear physics, fusion and gravitation. Some of the links are exact identities; others are analogies that still require testing. Each link is graded, followed by the national-security frame and the funding routes.

William Huanshan Chuang (莊桓山) · Logarchéon Inc.

Terms

  • Critical-entropy attention (CEAS): sets the "temperature" of each attention unit so its output stays near a phase transition, where it is most sensitive.
  • Ψ causal engine: answers cause-and-effect questions from data, and states when the data cannot decide (abstention).
  • GRAIL and MIA: attention computed from distances in hyperbolic space, so results are unchanged under that space's symmetries, including Lorentz boosts.
  • Two-phase scheme: methods learned on small models are applied to a large model by changing only a small set of control parameters.
  • Twin map; DL ≠ LD: a change of parameters that leaves a model's results unchanged; training does not commute with it ("DL ≠ LD"), so applying it changes how training proceeds.
  • Trace store; gate: a store that cross-checks spectra through trace identities, and a statistical test that accepts a change only when its benefit is bounded with stated confidence.

Standard of evidence

A link is listed only when a Logarchéon mechanism has a named counterpart in physics. Results proved or measured in Logarchéon's tests count for more than analogies. The work stays within open, unclassified science: nuclear structure and reactions, nuclear data, emulators, collision data and nonproliferation analytics.

Summary of findings

The overlap with nuclear physics is real but narrower than "almost everything". Several of these tools came from nuclear physics originally: random-matrix theory, for example, was first used for nuclear energy levels. The most promising contributions are those already proved or measured:

  1. DL ≠ LD. Symmetry-generated transformations and preconditioning, with a proved bound on error amplification.
  2. Certified trace checks. The trace store as a check of semiclassical shell structure.
  3. Identification with abstention. The causal engine for nuclear data evaluation.
  4. Gated, traceable updates. The gate and hash chain as verification and provenance.
  5. Exact Lorentz invariance. GRAIL/MIA for collision data.
  6. Compression. Large models on one processor, on site.

DL ≠ LD: tunnelling in the Logarchéon sense

In Logarchéon, tunnelling is the twin jump and its non-commutation with training. Let D be a gradient step and L a twin map, an invertible map with ℓ(Lθ) = ℓ(θ). The following identity is proved:

D(Lθ) − L·D(θ) = η (L − L−⊤) ∇ℓ(θ)

The commutator vanishes exactly when L is orthogonal; for a Lorentz boost of rapidity φ its size is 2 sinh φ. A twin jump reaches a point of equal loss at no cost, and training continues from there under the preconditioner (LᵀL)⁻¹. Mapped back, a boost amplifies error by at most eφ.

Three nuclear counterparts:

  1. Transforming before an approximate solve. Ab initio theory applies transformations that keep exact energies but speed the truncated calculation. Unitary ones are the orthogonal case; non-unitary ones are the boosts, and the eφ bound is a guarantee they lack. Bosonic Bogoliubov transformations have the boost's form, u = cosh r, v = sinh r.
  2. Variational wavefunctions for light nuclei. Neural-network wavefunctions need a good preconditioner (stochastic reconfiguration). A twin jump is a preconditioner change obtained from a symmetry.
  3. Fission paths. The action S = ∫√(2M(q)(V − E)) ds does not depend on the shape coordinates, but gradient-based path searches do unless they use the inertia tensor M as metric. The covariant transport rule is that requirement; M plays the role of LᵀL.

Status. The commutator is classical: it changes how descent moves, not barrier penetration with amplitude e−S. On small character models, twin jumps changed the final loss by under 0.001. The claim to test is symmetry-generated preconditioning or transformation with a proved bound.

From language to nuclei: what each stage means

In a language model the units are words. Here the units are nuclei: each token is a nucleus (proton number Z, neutron number N), one of its energy levels, or a reaction. The stages then read as follows.

  1. Data. Measured masses, levels and cross sections become the sequence the model reads, in place of text.
  2. Geometry (GRAIL). Every token and every parameter is a point in hyperbolic space, and the model's scores are distances between points. Training moves points, so the loss is a landscape over that geometry. With a discrete group Γ, it is a landscape on the quotient Γ\ℍⁿ, a finite space with the symmetries built in.
  3. Twin maps (Lorentz group). Moving all points by the same symmetry leaves every prediction unchanged. This is like changing the basis or coordinates in a nuclear calculation: the physics is the same, but how the calculation converges is not (DL ≠ LD).
  4. Laplace eigenfunctions: the normal modes of the landscape. Any function on the landscape, the loss included, can be written as a sum of the geometry's normal modes, as a vibration is written as a sum of harmonics. The eigenvalue says how fast a mode varies. Low modes carry broad structure across the whole dataset; high modes carry local detail. For nuclear data, the hypothesis is that smooth trends across the chart of nuclides fall in the low modes and shell effects in higher ones. This is untested.
  5. Hecke operators: extra conserved labels. A Hecke operator T_p averages each point with p + 1 related points of the geometry. It commutes with the Laplacian. In a nucleus, angular momentum and parity commute with the energy, so states of equal energy are told apart by those quantum numbers. In the same way, Hecke eigenvalues tell apart modes that the Laplacian alone cannot. The joint Hecke–Laplace eigenfunctions are the landscape's modes with all their labels: its "quantum numbers".
  6. Langlands: global access to the spectrum. By the Langlands correspondence and the trace formula, these labels and frequencies match arithmetic data: counts of closed geodesics, traces of matrices, values at primes. The whole spectrum of the landscape can therefore be read from counting, without exploring the landscape point by point. Training can then fit nuclear data by choosing the coefficient of each labelled mode, solved globally, instead of by many small gradient steps.
  7. Two phases. Modes and programs found on light nuclei, where exact calculations exist, are applied to heavy nuclei by changing only a small set of control parameters.

Status on a 32-billion-parameter model. Stages 1–3 are built and tested on language data. On an open-weight language model of 32.8 billion parameters, held at 2.6 bits per weight in 11.8 GB of memory:

  • all 4,096 attention heads were lifted into hyperbolic geometry with no retraining, at a cost of 0.6% or less in perplexity up to moderate curvature (16.84 → 16.79–16.95);
  • at the strongest curvature tested, perplexity rose to 40.17, and 60 steps of training on the control set brought it back to 18.14; the statistical gate has not yet accepted this, and nearly all of the remaining cost lies in the early layers;
  • Hecke attention ran on lifted heads at a cost of about 0.2% before any training;
  • the CEAS temperature program, accepted by the gate, recovered about 5% of the loss from compression, and a fact update through an adapter passed checks on unrelated text and on 200 general-knowledge questions.

Stages 4–7 on nuclear data are untested. Hecke operators exist only when Γ is arithmetic.

Gravitation (gr-qc)

Gravity in 2+1 dimensions (AdS₃, whose Euclidean form is hyperbolic 3-space) gives exact links, including the Hecke side that has no nuclear use. Logarchéon works with the Lorentz group O(n−1,1) in any dimension, so the image-sum and trace-formula links extend to higher-dimensional AdS quotients and to 3+1 cosmology; the Hecke links are specific to AdS₃.

National security within open science

The national-security value lies in open lanes that do not need a clearance:

  • uncertainty quantification of nuclear data, on which reactors and national programmes depend (the nuclear-data working group is interagency);
  • nonproliferation detection analytics and safeguards;
  • nuclear forensics attribution, where identification with abstention fits;
  • trustworthy, verified AI: gated, traceable updates.

Boundary. Open nuclear-structure and reaction data are unrestricted. Technical assistance on reactors or enrichment can fall under 10 CFR 810 and export controls; weapons-related work is classified. Work stays on open data, and a national-laboratory partner advises on any boundary.

First experiments

Each experiment is preregistered, uses public data and is compared with published baselines.

  1. Twin jump in variational training of a light nucleus (for example ⁴He with a neural-network wavefunction). One GPU. Tests DL ≠ LD directly; the strongest preliminary result for a proposal.
  2. Ψ abstention on optical-model fits to public cross sections (EXFOR).
  3. Trace-formula check of shell corrections against exact spectra.
  4. Spacing statistics on evaluated level schemes (ENSDF), with the gate deciding flags.
  5. Lorentz-exact classifier on simulated Electron-Ion Collider events, against LorentzNet and PELICAN.

Results will be posted on arXiv (nucl-th, gr-qc) with code, and used as preliminary results for a DOE Phase I proposal with a national-laboratory co-investigator.
